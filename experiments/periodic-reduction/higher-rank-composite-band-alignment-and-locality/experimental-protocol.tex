\section{Property-by-construction grammar and guarantee ledger}

The common family is a finite-dimensional design grammar. For a declared finite rank
$r$, choose a periodic Hermitian retained field
$K_{\mathrm{ret}}(\mathbf k)\in\mathbb C^{r\times r}$, a periodic Hermitian exterior
field $D_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf k)\in\mathbb C^{r\times r}$, and a periodic ambient
unitary $W(\mathbf k)\in U(2r)$. The fields are admissible only when their spectra obey
the certified ordering

\begin{equation}
 \inf_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb T^d}
 \left[
 \lambda_{\min}(D_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf k))
 -\lambda_{\max}(K_{\mathrm{ret}}(\mathbf k))
 \right]
 \geq\Delta_{\mathrm{iso}}>0.
 \label{eq:general-construction-gap}
\end{equation}

With the canonical embedding from the preceding section, define

\begin{equation}
 F(\mathbf k)=W(\mathbf k)E_r,
 \qquad
 P(\mathbf k)=F(\mathbf k)F^\dagger(\mathbf k),
 \label{eq:general-construction-frame}
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
 H_p(\mathbf k)=W(\mathbf k)
 \begin{pmatrix}
 K_{\mathrm{ret}}(\mathbf k)&0\\
 0&D_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf k)
 \end{pmatrix}
 W^\dagger(\mathbf k).
 \label{eq:general-construction-parent}
\end{equation}

These definitions guarantee before numerical sampling that

\begin{equation}
 F^\dagger F=I_r,
 \qquad
 P^2=P=P^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}P=r,
 \qquad
 F^\dagger H_pF=K_{\mathrm{ret}},
 \label{eq:general-construction-guarantees}
\end{equation}

that the parent spectrum is the multiset union of the retained and exterior spectra,
and that the exterior isolation energy is at least $\Delta_{\mathrm{iso}}$. Periodicity,
continuity, smoothness, analyticity, or a declared symmetry are guaranteed only to the
extent that the selected input fields satisfy the corresponding exact constraints.

A further periodic internal gauge $G(\mathbf k)\in U(r)$ gives

\begin{equation}
 F'(\mathbf k)=F(\mathbf k)G(\mathbf k),
 \qquad
 H_{F'}(\mathbf k)=G^\dagger(\mathbf k)
 K_{\mathrm{ret}}(\mathbf k)G(\mathbf k),
 \label{eq:general-construction-gauge}
\end{equation}

with exact identities

\begin{equation}
 F'F^{\prime\dagger}=P,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{spec}H_{F'}=\operatorname{spec}K_{\mathrm{ret}}.
 \label{eq:general-construction-gauge-guarantees}
\end{equation}

The construction inputs have distinct scientific roles:

\begin{itemize}

D_r(\mathbf k)=\operatorname{diag}

\Delta_{\mathrm{iso}}=\inf_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb T^d}

H_p^{(d,r)}(\mathbf k)=W_r(\mathbf k)
\begin{pmatrix}

0&D_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf k)
\end{pmatrix}

\qquad
P_r(\mathbf k)=F_r(\mathbf k)F_r^\dagger(\mathbf k).

The smooth represented operator is exactly

H_{F_r}(\mathbf k)=F_r^\dagger H_p^{(d,r)}F_r=D_r(\mathbf k).

\section{Layered Givens gauge decomposition}

For adjacent retained coordinates $j$ and $j+1$, define the embedded complex Givens

\[
R_{j,j+1}(\theta,\phi)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}_{j,j+1}.
\]

At layer $\ell$, use the disjoint neighbor pairs

\[
\mathcal P_\ell=
\left\{(j,j+1):1\leq j<r,\ j\equiv\ell\pmod 2\right\}.
\]

Let every $\theta_{\ell j}(\mathbf k)$, $\phi_{\ell j}(\mathbf k)$, and

\[
Q_r(\mathbf k)=\operatorname{diag}
\left(e^{i\chi_1(\mathbf k)},\ldots,e^{i\chi_r(\mathbf k)}\right)
\]

and

\[
G_{r,L}(\mathbf k)=Q_r(\mathbf k)
\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}
\prod_{(j,j+1)\in\mathcal P_\ell}
R_{j,j+1}\!\left(
\theta_{\ell j}(\mathbf k),\phi_{\ell j}(\mathbf k)
\right).
\]

Increasing layer order is part of the canonical definition. Factors within a layer
commute; adjacent layers generally do not. The interaction graph is connected once the
selected layers cover every adjacent pair, so the construction is not a direct sum of
independent rank-two attacks.

The protocol distinguishes two gauge classes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{G0, real class:} $\phi_{\ell j}=0$ and $\chi_a=0$, hence
$G_{r,L}\in SO(r)$;
\item \textbf{G1, complex class:} complex pair phases and diagonal phases are allowed, hence
$G_{r,L}\in U(r)$.
\end{itemize}

A G0 result does not validate G1. The attacked frame and represented operator are

\[
F'_{r,L}(\mathbf k)=F_r(\mathbf k)G_{r,L}(\mathbf k),
\qquad
H'_{r,L}(\mathbf k)=G_{r,L}^\dagger(\mathbf k)
D_r(\mathbf k)G_{r,L}(\mathbf k).

The defining exact identities are

\[
F_{r,L}^{\prime\dagger}F'_{r,L}=I_r,
\qquad
F'_{r,L}F_{r,L}^{\prime\dagger}=P_r,
\qquad
\operatorname{spec}H'_{r,L}=\operatorname{spec}D_r.

\section{Alignment families and exact application contract}

Every comparison declares its admissible alignment family before observing results. Let

\begin{equation}
 R(\mathbf k)=F_r(\mathbf k),
 \qquad
 C(\mathbf k)=F'_{r,L}(\mathbf k),
 \qquad
 H_C(\mathbf k)=H'_{r,L}(\mathbf k)
 \label{eq:alignment-reference-candidate}
\end{equation}

be the reference frame, attacked candidate frame, and represented candidate operator.
Every selected alignment acts on candidate coordinates from the right. For a unitary
$U(\mathbf k)\in U(r)$, define

\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_U(\mathbf k)=C(\mathbf k)U(\mathbf k),
 \qquad
 \widehat H_U(\mathbf k)=U^\dagger(\mathbf k)
 H_C(\mathbf k)U(\mathbf k).
 \label{eq:alignment-application}
\end{equation}

This source/target orientation is canonical for D1 v1. Returning $U^\dagger$ instead of
$U$ while leaving Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-application} unchanged is a convention
failure, not an equivalent serialization.

For a finite ordered sample set $\mathcal S$, the one-unitary normalized frame objective
is

\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathcal S}(U)
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|r}
 \sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal S}
 \lVert C(\mathbf k)U-R(\mathbf k)\rVert_F^2,
 \qquad U\in U(r).
 \label{eq:alignment-global-objective}
\end{equation}

Equal raw sample weights are mandatory. The normalization does not change the minimizer
but is part of the reported objective. Define pointwise cross-covariances and their
ordered equal-weight aggregate by

\begin{equation}
 M(\mathbf k)=C^\dagger(\mathbf k)R(\mathbf k),
 \qquad
 B_{\mathcal S}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|}
 \sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal S}M(\mathbf k).
 \label{eq:alignment-cross-covariances}
\end{equation}

For either $X=M(\mathbf k)$ or $X=B_{\mathcal S}$, compute the complete square complex
SVD

\begin{equation}
 X=L\Sigma V^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r),
 \qquad
 \sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_r\geq0.
 \label{eq:alignment-svd}
\end{equation}

Only when $\sigma_r>\tau_{\mathrm{sv}}(X)$ is the selected polar factor

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P(X)=LV^\dagger
 \label{eq:alignment-polar-factor}
\end{equation}

admitted. It uniquely minimizes the applicable square-unitary Procrustes objective. The
implementation records every singular value, the applied cutoff, the strict margin
$\sigma_r-\tau_{\mathrm{sv}}(X)$, and
$\kappa_2(X)=\sigma_1/\sigma_r$. No finite condition number is reported when the strict
rank gate fails. Repeated positive singular values do not make the polar factor
nonunique, although the individual SVD factors may be nonunique.

The selected diagnostic ladder is:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A0, identity:}
$U_{A0}(\mathbf k)=I_r$. No transformation is selected; the attacked representation is
measured as supplied.

\item \textbf{A1, one global unitary:}
$U_{A1}=\mathcal P(B_{\mathcal S})$ minimizes
Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-global-objective} over exactly one $U\in U(r)$. D1 v1 uses
$\mathcal S=\mathcal K_{2049}$, evaluates the ordered sum in increasing mesh-index order,
and reuses the selected $U_{A1}$ on every other construction and evaluation point. It
is not refitted on another mesh.

\item \textbf{A2, pointwise complete unitary:}
$U_{A2}(\mathbf k)=\mathcal P(M(\mathbf k))$ independently minimizes
$\lVert C(\mathbf k)U-R(\mathbf k)\rVert_F^2/r$ at every construction point. D1 v1
reconstructs A2 separately on each construction mesh. It does not interpolate selected
unitaries onto the withheld mesh; the withheld direct reference for the recovered field
is the analytic $D_r$ declared below.

\item \textbf{A3, known inverse oracle:}
$U_{A3}(\mathbf k)=G_{r,L}^{-1}(\mathbf k)=G_{r,L}^\dagger(\mathbf k)$. A3 performs no
SVD or optimization. It verifies construction, source/target orientation, application,
and Fourier conventions only.

\item \textbf{A4, restricted circuit:} a separately frozen depth- and harmonic-bounded
Givens family, if a reviewed optimizer and certificate policy are later selected. A4 is
not part of D1 v1.
\end{enumerate}

A1, A2, and A4 are different feasible sets. None is described as a numerical
approximation to another. The oracle A3 is not an admissible fitted solution and cannot
be used to claim practical recoverability. In the exact D1 P0 construction,
$M(k)=G_{r,L}^\dagger(k)$, so A2 and A3 coincide analytically; their numerical separation
is retained solely as an implementation check.

For every admitted Procrustes factor, the producer recomputes the objective from the
original $C$ and $R$ inputs rather than only from singular values. It also records

\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{unit}}(U)=\lVert U^\dagger U-I_r\rVert_F,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(X,U)
 =\frac{\lVert U^\dagger X-X^\dagger U\rVert_F}{\sqrt r}.
 \label{eq:alignment-integrity-defects}
\end{equation}

For A1, the covariance expression for Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-global-objective}
is

\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathrm{cov}}(X,U)
 =2-\frac{2}{r}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(X^\dagger U),
 \label{eq:alignment-objective-covariance}
\end{equation}

with $X=B_{\mathcal S}$; the same expression with $X=M(\mathbf k)$ is the pointwise A2
objective. Let $J_{\mathrm{direct}}$ denote the value recomputed from the original frame
residuals in Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-global-objective}, or its one-point A2 form.
The remaining integrity diagnostics are

\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{obj}}
 =\left|J_{\mathrm{direct}}-J_{\mathrm{cov}}\right|,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{psd}}
 =\max\!\left\{0,-\lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 \frac{U^\dagger X+X^\dagger U}{2}
 \right)\right\}.
 \label{eq:alignment-objective-psd-defects}
\end{equation}

q=\frac{\sqrt{41}-3}{8},
\qquad
s_*=\sqrt{1-q^2}\left(\frac34+\frac q2\right).
\]

the maximizing cosine is $q$ for even $\ell$ and $-q$ for odd $\ell$. The positive
amplitude factor is largest at $j=r-1$. Hence

\[
\max_{\substack{k\in[-\pi,\pi)\\ \ell\geq1,\ 1\leq j<r}}
|\theta_{\ell j}(k)|
=0.35\left[1+0.15\frac{r-2}{r-1}\right]s_*<0.4025\ \mathrm{rad}.
\]

This is an analytic continuum certificate, unlike the sampled development diagnostic
below. Layers use the canonical odd/even pair sets and increasing product order defined
above. One layer is an intentionally incomplete-connectivity baseline; two or more
layers cover every adjacent edge and generate the noncommuting connected-chain control.

\subsection{Exact representation certificates}

The smooth represented field $D_r(k)$ is a trigonometric polynomial with harmonics only
at $0$, $1$, and $2$. Under the continuum Fourier convention of this protocol, its
blocks therefore satisfy

\[

\]

This is an analytic support statement, not a sampled locality result. By contrast,
entries of $G_{r,L}(k)$ contain sines and cosines of nonconstant trigonometric
polynomials. Consequently the attacked field
$H'_{r,L}(k)=G_{r,L}^{\dagger}(k)D_r(k)G_{r,L}(k)$ is periodic and analytic but is not
assigned any finite Fourier support by the construction. The first-two-harmonic input
inventory must not be mistaken for a hopping cutoff after the nonlinear gauge attack.

For P0, the exact candidate-to-reference cross-overlap is

\[

Every singular value of $M(k)$ is exactly one. The unique complete-unitary pointwise
polar factor is therefore $G_{r,L}^{\dagger}(k)=G_{r,L}^{-1}(k)$. In exact arithmetic,

finite-grid agreement between them tests implementation and convention only; it is not
new scientific evidence. No corresponding recovery statement applies to A1, whose
aggregate cross-covariance must independently pass its rank threshold.

Every represented field is pointwise Hermitian and periodic, so its continuum Fourier

\subsection{Periodic 1-D}

The one-dimensional case uses the same sequential parallel-transport and distributed
closure-correction convention as the Part~2 rank-two workflow, generalized to rank $r$
and using the D1 maximum-rule rank cutoff. Let $Y_n=F_r(k_n)$ be the directly sampled
analytic reference frame, set $\widetilde F_0=Y_0$, and append the direct endpoint
$Y_M=F_r(+\pi)$ after the $M$ half-open samples. In increasing index order define
\[
 O_n=\widetilde F_n^\dagger Y_{n+1}=L_n\Sigma_nR_n^\dagger,
 \qquad Q_n=R_nL_n^\dagger,
 \qquad \widetilde F_{n+1}=Y_{n+1}Q_n .
\]
Equivalently, $Q_n$ is the unique full-rank right-unitary Procrustes transformation from
$Y_{n+1}$ to $\widetilde F_n$. Every neighbor step and the endpoint step use the strict
cutoff $\tau_{\mathrm{sv}}$ already selected for D1; no deficient null-space completion is
permitted. Sequential transport stops at the first deficient required link, retains that
evaluated deficient index and the exact ordered indices left unattempted, and does not
infer rank outcomes for links whose transported source frame is consequently unavailable.

Let the endpoint mismatch be $S=\widetilde F_0^\dagger\widetilde F_M$ and let the unitary
polar factor of $S$ have the spectral representation
$Z\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\gamma_1},\ldots,e^{i\gamma_r})Z^\dagger$ with principal phases
in $(-\pi,\pi]$. The periodically closed frame is
\[
 F_n^{\mathrm{pt}}=\widetilde F_n
 Z\operatorname{diag}\!\left(e^{-i(n/M)\gamma_1},\ldots,
 e^{-i(n/M)\gamma_r}\right)Z^\dagger,
 \qquad n=0,\ldots,M.
\]
The ordered closure product therefore follows the same increasing-index traversal rather
than an independently chosen link orientation. Endpoint frame and projector sewing are
evaluated directly from $F_r(-\pi)$, $F_r(+\pi)$, $P_r(-\pi)$, and $P_r(+\pi)$.
Transport acts on the analytic reference frame and is shared by A0--A3; attacked-frame
behavior remains in the separate alignment and recovery diagnostics. For P0 the exact
reference frame is constant, but the complete transport route is still executed rather
than replaced by a fabricated zero.

The one-dimensional case has one neighbor-link family, one primitive reciprocal closure,
and hopping blocks indexed by $R\in\mathbb Z$. It is the primary rank/depth isolation
case because it introduces no plaquette channel. Closure eigenphases are stored as an
unordered rank-$r$ multiset.

\section{Topological boundary}

The analytic frame $F_r(\mathbf k)=W_r(\mathbf k)E_r$ is global and periodic. Therefore
P0 and P1 define topologically trivial complex composite bundles. Wilson loops, closure
phases, and plaquettes are transport and sewing diagnostics in this family; they are not
claims of Chern, $\mathbb Z_2$, crystalline, or three-dimensional topological character.

A nonidentity Wilson loop does not by itself imply a topological obstruction. A future
obstructed model would require patchwise frames and transition functions, and would be a
separate experiment family rather than a hidden coefficient choice here.

The reusable diagnostics developed from this family may later support a fail-closed
pristine/defect compatibility screen, but Paper 3 does not construct defect operators.
Paper 5 owns the common-space subtraction decision, and Paper 6 owns full topological
and symmetry-obstruction protocols. The synthetic P0/P1 cases exercise known-compatible
frames; they do not validate material bundle compatibility.

\section{Diagnostic definitions and ledger}

For a channel $A$ and construction mesh $M$, write $T_{A,M}(R)$ for the complete block
and $H_{A,M}(k)$ for its full interpolant. For support $\Omega_S$, retain both

\[

\qquad
N_{A,M}(S)=\sqrt{W_{A,M}(S)},
\]

and the block-$\ell^1$ tail

\[
L_{A,M}(S)=\sum_{R\notin\Omega_S}\|T_{A,M}(R)\|_F.
\]

Report $W$ raw and divided by $rE_{\mathrm{ref}}^2$; report $N$ and $L$ raw and divided
by $\sqrt rE_{\mathrm{ref}}$. These are complete-matrix quantities, not sums of selected
orbital entries.

For every construction mesh, channel, and support, the discrete Parseval identity is

\begin{equation}
 \frac1M\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_M}
 \left\lVert H_{A,M}^{(S)}(k)-H_{A,M}(k)\right\rVert_F^2
 =W_{A,M}(S).
 \label{eq:diagnostic-parseval}
\end{equation}

The producer retains both sides and the relative defect defined by the frozen denominator
rule. Since the supports are nested, it also verifies that $W$, $N$, and $L$ are
nonincreasing as $S$ increases. For each successive support pair it records the positive
increase after dividing $W$ by $rE_{\mathrm{ref}}^2$ and $N,L$ by
$\sqrt rE_{\mathrm{ref}}$; each increase must pass the selected dimensionless
support-monotonicity tolerance. A monotonicity failure is an integrity failure, not
evidence for nonmonotone locality.

$M\in\{513,1025,2049\}$:

 &=H_{A,M}(k)-H_A^{\mathrm{direct}}(k),\\
e_{\mathrm{trunc},A,M}^{(S)}(k)
 &=H_{A,M}^{(S)}(k)-H_{A,M}(k),\\
e_{\mathrm{total},A,M}^{(S)}(k)
 &=H_{A,M}^{(S)}(k)-H_A^{\mathrm{direct}}(k).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:diagnostic-error-channels}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 e_{\mathrm{total},A,M}^{(S)}

\end{equation}

\textbf{resolved global-alignment residual} only if its maximum normalized represented-
operator residual is at least $10^{-6}$; otherwise it is unresolved at v1 resolution.
No ratio with a roundoff-scale denominator is used.

Rank or depth differences are descriptive unless the absolute difference between two
normalized diagnostics exceeds both $10^{-6}$ and ten times their larger measured
refinement change. Monotonicity is not assumed, and the finite ladder supports no
asymptotic-in-rank or asymptotic-in-depth law. A failure to resolve an effect is not a
negative theorem.

\section{Support--error Pareto diagnostics}

D1 adopts Part~2's sampled nondominance rule while keeping the higher-rank alignment
families separate. For each technically qualified case, applicable channel $A$, and
final construction mesh $M=2049$, every declared support $S$ defines the three-objective
point
\[
 \mathcal J_{r,L,A}(S)=\left(
 |\Omega_S|,
 \varepsilon_{H,r,L,A}^{\mathrm{total}}(S),
 \varepsilon_{E,r,L,A}^{\mathrm{total}}(S)
 \right),
\]
where $|\Omega_S|=2S+1$, $\varepsilon_H^{\mathrm{total}}$ is the withheld-mesh maximum
total Frobenius error divided by $\sqrt rE_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and
$\varepsilon_E^{\mathrm{total}}$ is the withheld-mesh maximum total ordered-spectrum
error divided by $E_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Lower values of all three components are preferred.
A sampled support is Pareto efficient exactly when no other declared support in the same
case and channel weakly improves all three components and strictly improves at least one.

Channels are never pooled into one Pareto set: A0, A1, A2, and A3 are distinct feasible
alignment families rather than scale samples of one family. A1 has no Pareto record
when its aggregate alignment is rank deficient. A2 and A3 Pareto records are explicitly
control diagnostics; in particular, the known-inverse A3 oracle is not an attainable
fitted decision and cannot support a practical-selection claim. Pareto membership uses
only technically qualified final-mesh values, selects no preferred support, applies no
acceptance tolerance, and asserts neither interpolation between supports nor a continuum
front. The producer retains all objective triples and Boolean membership values, and
the independent verifier reconstructs the dominance relation directly.

This diagnostic is additional to, not a replacement for, the fixed $S=2$ claim-resolution
rules, complete support ledger, or $1025\rightarrow2049$ refinement checks. A
componentwise envelope assembled from different supports is not an attainable decision
and is never serialized as a Pareto representative.
