\begin{abstract}
This protocol selects a silicon-inspired synthetic parent on the diamond lattice: an
FCC Bravais lattice in a non-orthogonal primitive basis with a two-site tetrahedral
basis.  It is the three-dimensional non-orthogonal extension of the
publication-independent experiment \emph{Band Equivalence versus Hopping Locality}.  A
separate matched simple-cubic--FCC Bravais control isolates metric and bond-orbit
behavior without being identified as silicon.  The inspired parent contains two sites
and two abstract orbital channels per cell; it is not an $sp^3s^*$ model or a material
parametrization.  Complete reference and rotated rank-two fields are pointwise unitarily
equivalent before truncation.  The primary outcome is the additional metric- and
point-group-complete hopping support required by the rotated silicon-inspired
representation at a fixed withheld band-error target.

The six positive FCC bond vectors have several integer relations.  Their flat-parent
correlation is therefore a constrained three-index Bessel sum, not the simple-cubic
product of independent $J_0$ factors.  The protocol independently checks this
comparator, the residual--non-onsite-weight identity, three-dimensional finite Fourier
conversion, metric-shell construction, primitive-basis covariance, and resource-safe
execution.  Separately authorized bounded local Monkhorst--Pack actions at $N=8,16,32$
now exercise these channels; they are not an immutable version, material claim,
qualified convergence result, or continuum certificate.

\section{FCC primitive and matched simple-cubic geometry}

Let $a_c$ be the conventional cubic length.  The FCC primitive matrix and Gram matrix
are
\begin{equation}
 A_{\mathrm{fcc}}=\frac{a_c}{2}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&1\\
 1&0&1\\
 1&1&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 G_{\mathrm{fcc}}=A_{\mathrm{fcc}}^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm{fcc}}
 =\frac{a_c^2}{4}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2&1&1\\
 1&2&1\\
 1&1&2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:fcc-metric}
\end{equation}
The reciprocal primitive matrix is
$B_{\mathrm{fcc}}=2\pi A_{\mathrm{fcc}}^{-\mathsf T}$.  Reduced coordinates satisfy
$\mathbf q\in[-1/2,1/2)^3$, physical wavevectors are
$\mathbf k=B_{\mathrm{fcc}}\mathbf q$, and the Bloch phase is
$e^{2\pi i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}$.

Choose the positive half of the FCC nearest-neighbor orbit as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_{\mathrm{fcc},+}
 =\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3,
     \mathbf e_2-\mathbf e_1,
     \mathbf e_3-\mathbf e_1,
     \mathbf e_3-\mathbf e_2\},
 \qquad m_{\mathrm{fcc}}=6.
 \label{eq:fcc-bonds}
\end{equation}
Together with its negatives it contains twelve bonds of length $a_c/\sqrt2$.  The
matched simple-cubic control uses a nearest-neighbor length $a_c/\sqrt2$ so that its
physical nearest-neighbor distance equals the FCC value:
\begin{equation}
 A_{\mathrm{sc}}=\frac{a_c}{\sqrt2}I_3,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_{\mathrm{sc},+}=\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3\},
 \qquad m_{\mathrm{sc}}=3.
\end{equation}
This length matching does not make the cell volumes or dispersions equal; those remain
reported geometry properties.

For $X\in\{\mathrm{sc},\mathrm{fcc}\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 r_X^2(\mathbf R)=\mathbf R^{\mathsf T}G_X\mathbf R,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_X=\{M\in GL(3,\Z):M^{\mathsf T}G_XM=G_X\}.
 \label{eq:metric-and-group}
\end{equation}
A convenient integer FCC shell index is
\begin{equation}
 s_{\mathrm{fcc}}(\mathbf R)
 =\frac{2r_{\mathrm{fcc}}^2(\mathbf R)}{a_c^2}
 =R_1^2+R_2^2+R_3^2+R_1R_2+R_1R_3+R_2R_3.
 \label{eq:fcc-shell-index}
\end{equation}
Nearest neighbors have $s_{\mathrm{fcc}}=1$.  Support inventories must contain complete
$\Gamma_X$ orbits and every attainable metric shell through the cap.  Coordinate radius
$R_1^2+R_2^2+R_3^2$ is not an FCC physical shell label.

PhysKit owns reusable direct, reciprocal, metric, and finite-domain lattice primitives.
This experiment owns the selected FCC orbit, basis semantics, support acceptance,
thresholds, verification, and evidence policy.

\subsection{Diamond basis and silicon-inspired connectivity}

The FCC Bravais lattice becomes diamond only after adding two tetrahedrally related
basis sites.  In reduced direct coordinates, select
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\tau_A=(0,0,0),
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\tau_B=(1/4,1/4,1/4).
 \label{eq:diamond-basis}
\end{equation}
The four nearest $A$--$B$ bonds use cell shifts
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{AB}=\{\mathbf0,-\mathbf e_1,-\mathbf e_2,-\mathbf e_3\},
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\delta_j=A_{\mathrm{fcc}}
 (\boldsymbol\ell_j+\boldsymbol\tau_B-\boldsymbol\tau_A).
 \label{eq:diamond-bonds}
\end{equation}
All four physical vectors have length $a_c\sqrt3/4$.  In the periodic orbital
convention, define the normalized diamond structure factor
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)
 =\frac14\left[1+e^{-2\pi iq_1}+e^{-2\pi iq_2}+e^{-2\pi iq_3}\right],
 \qquad |\gamma_{\mathrm{dia}}|\le1.
 \label{eq:diamond-structure-factor}
\end{equation}
This encodes a diamond-like bipartite connectivity only.  It does not encode silicon
valleys, $sp^3s^*$ orbitals, spin--orbit coupling, or first-principles matrix elements.

\section{Matched Bravais-control parents}

Here and below $E_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ is an arbitrary reference-energy normalization, not
a band gap. Use the common onsite matrix
\begin{equation}
 T_0=E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1.20&0.06&0&0\\
 0.06&-0.35&0&0\\
 0&0&1.00&0.04\\
 0&0&0.04&1.75
 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and the Hermitian orbital coupling
\begin{equation}
 V=E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0.30&0&0.12&0\\
 0&-0.20&0&0.10\\
 0.12&0&0.10&0\\
 0&0.10&0&-0.08
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:orbital-coupling}
\end{equation}
The same $V$ is assigned to each unoriented bond, giving a trivial internal action of
the lattice point group.  This defines a matched-control parent distinct from the
directed-hopping simple-cubic protocol; its numerical outcomes are not inherited.

For $0\le\rho\le1$, define
\begin{align}
 f_X(\mathbf q)
 &=\frac1{m_X}\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
   \cos(2\pi\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf b),\\
 K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)&=T_0+\rho f_X(\mathbf q)V.
 \label{eq:parent}
\end{align}
Then $f_X(\mathbf0)=1$ and $|f_X|\le1$.  This fixes a common extremal scale, not equal
cell volumes, densities of states, or curvatures.

For construction size $N$, set
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_{\mathbf b}
 =\mathsf S_N^{b_1}\otimes\mathsf S_N^{b_2}\otimes\mathsf S_N^{b_3}.
\end{equation}
The finite cell--orbital parent on
$\mathcal H_{p,N}^{(3)}=(\mathbb C^N)^{\otimes3}\otimes\mathbb C^4$ is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat K_{\rho,X,N}
 =I_{N^3}\otimes T_0
 +\frac{\rho}{2m_X}\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
  (\mathsf S_{\mathbf b}+\mathsf S_{\mathbf b}^{\dagger})\otimes V.
 \label{eq:finite-parent}
\end{equation}
The tensor Fourier transform must reconstruct the direct sum of
$K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q_{ijk})$ without materializing a dense
$4N^3\times4N^3$ matrix.  Four orbitals occur per primitive cell.

Complete Hermitian fiber spectra define the lower rank-two projectors.  A qualified
global periodic frame gives
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)
 =F_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)F_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q).
\end{equation}
A conservative continuous enclosure must establish a positive band-two/band-three gap
for all retained $\rho$ and both geometries.  A sampled minimum is insufficient.  The
frame method must freeze its anchor, three-dimensional traversal, neighbor polar
alignment, three reciprocal-boundary sewing maps, path and plaquette checks, and rank
thresholds.  Failure gives an unresolved case, not a changed rank or gauge.

\section{Silicon-inspired dispersive parent}

The primary inspired parent uses the ordered basis
$(L,A),(L,B),(U,A),(U,B)$, where $A/B$ are diamond sites and $L/U$ are abstract lower
and upper orbital channels.  Define
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\gamma_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)\\
 \gamma_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)^*&0
 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 K_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\Delta I_2+\rho t_LS_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)&vI_2\\
 vI_2&+\Delta I_2+\rho t_US_{\mathrm{dia}}(\mathbf q)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:silicon-inspired-parent}
\end{equation}
where the superscript $\mathrm{Si}^*$ labels inspiration only, and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=1.20E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad t_L=0.30E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad t_U=0.10E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad v=0.08E_{\mathrm{ref}}.
\end{equation}
This two-channel construction supplies a rank-two low-energy group with diamond
connectivity while keeping two explicit spectator states.  Any implementation has model
role \texttt{TOY}; it is not a \texttt{SiliconReferenceModel}.  It contains no spin,
$s/p$ orbital representation, fitted silicon hopping, valley calibration, spin--orbit
coupling, or electron interaction.

Because $\norm{S_{\mathrm{dia}}}_2\le1$, a conservative Weyl bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_2
 \ge2\Delta-|t_L|-|t_U|-2|v|=1.84E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \label{eq:silicon-inspired-external-isolation-gap}
\end{equation}
for $0\le\rho\le1$.  The calculation must reconstruct the sampled gap and verify the
bound from exact input matrices.  Internal degeneracy of the retained pair is permitted
because the retained object is its rank-two projector.  A qualified periodic frame
defines
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)
 =F_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  K_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)F_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q).
\end{equation}

The finite parent replaces each exponential in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:diamond-structure-factor} by its exact cell-shift operator.  Its tensor
Fourier transform, full eigensystem, lower rank-two projector, periodic frame, projector
lift, and Wannier transform obey the same checks as the Bravais-control parents.  At
$\rho=0$ its retained pair is scalar, so the nontrivial flat residual--tail theorem is
tested by the separate constant-splitting Bravais control; it is not attributed to the
silicon-inspired endpoint.

\section{RMS-matched gauge family}

Let $g$ be the reciprocal-torus RMS rotation amplitude.  Define
\begin{align}
 \theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)
 &=g\sqrt{\frac{2}{m_X}}
   \sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
   \sin(2\pi\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf b),
 \label{eq:rms-phase}\\
 U_{g,X}(\mathbf q)&=R(\theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)),\\
 A_{\rho,g,X}(\mathbf q)
 &=U_{g,X}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
   H_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)U_{g,X}(\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:comparison}
\end{align}
For the silicon-inspired parent, use the FCC Bravais phase and define
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rho,g,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)
 =U_{g,\mathrm{fcc}}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  H_{\rho,\mathrm{Si}^*}(\mathbf q)U_{g,\mathrm{fcc}}(\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:silicon-inspired-comparison}
\end{equation}
Orthogonality of distinct bond characters gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\T^3}\theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)^2\,d\mathbf q=g^2.
\end{equation}
The envelope is $|\theta_{g,X}|\le a_X(g)=g\sqrt{2m_X}$.  Use the common primary range
\begin{equation}
 0\le g\le g_{\max}^{(3)}=\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{12}},
 \label{eq:g-range}
\end{equation}
so $a_X(g)\le\pi/4$ for both cases.  The realized maximum phase is reported separately.

Reference and rotated fields in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:comparison} and
\eqref{eq:silicon-inspired-comparison} have identical pointwise spectra.  Their
covariantly aligned subtractions under the applicable full $U_g(\mathbf q)$ vanish.  The restricted
scientific comparison permits only one momentum-independent real rotation $R(\phi)$.
The corresponding full Wannier-space unitary is generally nonlocal and is excluded from
the restricted channel.

\section{Flat-parent analytic comparators}

At $\rho=0$, fix the retained frame to the constant lower-two-eigenvector frame of
$T_0$.  For the simple-cubic control, put $x_{\mathrm{sc}}=2g\sqrt{2/3}$; character
independence gives
\begin{equation}
 c_{\mathrm{sc}}(g)=J_0(x_{\mathrm{sc}})^3.
 \label{eq:sc-comparator}
\end{equation}
This is the earlier $J_0(2a/3)^3$ law under the conversion
$a=g\sqrt6$.

For FCC, put $x_{\mathrm{fcc}}=2g/\sqrt3$.  Write the Bessel indices of the ordered
bonds in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fcc-bonds} as $n_1,\ldots,n_6$.  The zero-mode constraint is
\begin{align}
 n_1-n_4-n_5&=0,\\
 n_2+n_4-n_6&=0,\\
 n_3+n_5+n_6&=0.
\end{align}
Applying the Jacobi--Anger expansion~\cite{dlmf1012} and taking $p=n_4$, $q=n_5$,
and $r=n_6$ gives the exact comparator
\begin{equation}
 c_{\mathrm{fcc}}(g)
 =\sum_{p,q,r\in\Z}
 J_{p+q}(x_{\mathrm{fcc}})
 J_{r-p}(x_{\mathrm{fcc}})
 J_{-q-r}(x_{\mathrm{fcc}})
 J_p(x_{\mathrm{fcc}})J_q(x_{\mathrm{fcc}})J_r(x_{\mathrm{fcc}}).
 \label{eq:fcc-comparator}
\end{equation}
The producer must evaluate this series using a declared symmetric domain and reviewed
tail bound.  An independent high-accuracy torus quadrature must reproduce it.  Neither
calculation may silently replace the finite-mesh correlation stored with a run.

For $X\in\{\mathrm{sc},\mathrm{fcc}\}$, the flat-parent theorem predicts
\begin{align}
 \inf_{s,\lambda\ge0,\phi}L_{\mathrm{op},X,\phi}^2
 &=\kappa[1-c_X(g)^2]
 =\frac{1}{2E_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}\sum_{\mathbf R\ne\mathbf0}
   \norm{D_{\mathbf R}^{X}}_F^2,
 \label{eq:flat-tail}\\
 \inf_\phi L_{\mathrm{op},X,\phi}^2(0,1)
 &=2\kappa[1-c_X(g)].
\end{align}
The calculation must independently verify the analytic correlation, both residuals,
and shell-resolved Fourier weight.  No equality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:flat-tail} is assumed
for $\rho>0$.
