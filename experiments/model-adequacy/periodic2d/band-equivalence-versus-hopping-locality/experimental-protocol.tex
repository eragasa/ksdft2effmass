\begin{abstract}
This protocol defines a prospective two-dimensional extension of the
publication-independent experiment \emph{Band Equivalence versus Hopping Locality}. It
makes explicit the conventional lattice--orbital factorization that is implicit in the
1-D Bloch-field calculation. A square-lattice parent is constructed on
$(\mathbb C^N)^{\otimes2}\otimes\mathbb C^4$ with Kronecker translation operators. The
unitary tensor Fourier transform gives its complete Bloch representation; qualified
spectral projectors, a periodic retained frame, and a second Fourier transform then give
the complete rank-two Wannier/tight-binding representation. This construction aligns
state spaces before any Hamiltonian subtraction and distinguishes a spectral-basis
Hamiltonian from its eigenvalues alone.

The smooth family rotates the retained coordinates by
$\theta_a(\mathbf q)=\tfrac a2[\sin(2\pi q_x)+\sin(2\pi q_y)]$ while preserving every
Bloch eigenvalue. For the flat parent, the exact global-alignment correlation is
$J_0(a)^2$, and the irreducible matrix residual equals the normalized Fourier weight
outside the onsite block. The dispersive calculation will measure square-orbit support
at fixed sampled band accuracy while separately reporting parent--retained
reconstruction, covariant representation, restricted-alignment, cyclic conversion,
interpolation, deliberate-truncation, and total channels. Separately authorized bounded
Monkhorst--Pack actions at $N=16,32,64$ now exercise these channels; they are neither a
qualified convergence result, retained run, material claim, nor continuous-domain
certificate.

\section{Scientific question}

Band spectra are invariant under momentum-dependent unitary similarity, whereas a
finite-range hopping representation is not. Wannier gauge freedom and its effect on
real-space localization are established~\cite{marzari1997,marzari2012,marrazzo2024};
compact and maximally localized representatives need not coincide~\cite{sathe2024}, and
explicit hopping truncation can degrade both bands and response observables even when
band agreement appears satisfactory~\cite{ghosh2025}.

The experiment asks a narrower controlled question. Starting from one finite
square-lattice Hamiltonian, how do its complete Bloch-spectral, retained-frame, and
Wannier/tight-binding representations relate under explicit isometries, and how much
square-complete hopping support is required by two gauge-related retained
representations at the same sampled band-error target? The complete models are
physically equivalent under consistent transformation of states and operators. The
reported difference is a representation-cost comparison after truncation, not physical
inequivalence. Eigenvalue lists are used for spectral comparisons but are never treated
as operators and are never subtracted from Hamiltonian matrices.

The 2-D experiment is distinct from the scalar hopping-shell hierarchy. That experiment
studies coefficient-class expressivity for a scalar parent; this experiment studies
matrix-valued, exactly band-equivalent representations and their post-truncation band
consequences.

\section{Square lattice and reciprocal convention}

The direct basis is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf a_1=a_0\mathbf e_x,
 \qquad
 \mathbf a_2=a_0\mathbf e_y,
\end{equation}
and the reciprocal basis is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf b_1=\frac{2\pi}{a_0}\mathbf e_x,
 \qquad
 \mathbf b_2=\frac{2\pi}{a_0}\mathbf e_y.
\end{equation}
Reduced coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf q=(q_x,q_y)\in[-1/2,1/2)^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbf k=q_x\mathbf b_1+q_y\mathbf b_2.
\end{equation}
A displacement is $\mathbf R=(R_x,R_y)\in\Z^2$, and the Bloch phase is
$e^{2\pi i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}$. Energies are reported in one arbitrary fixed
positive reference unit $E_{\mathrm{ref}}$; this symbol is not a band gap.

The parent and rotation family preserve coordinate exchange and global reciprocal
inversion. Global inversion acts with complex conjugation on the real-orbital Bloch
matrix. The protocol does not assume equality of hopping coefficients under independent
axis sign changes. Square-complete truncation supports are invariant under all signed
coordinate permutations, but support symmetry must not be confused with coefficient
symmetry.

\section{Finite spectral and tight-binding realizations}

For a declared construction size $N$, define the lattice, parent, and retained spaces
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_N^{(2)}=(\mathbb C^N)^{\otimes2},\qquad
 \mathcal H_{p,N}^{(2)}=\mathcal L_N^{(2)}\otimes\mathbb C^4,\qquad
 \mathcal H_{r,N}^{(2)}=\mathcal L_N^{(2)}\otimes\mathbb C^2.
 \label{eq:spaces}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathsf F_N$ be the unitary centered discrete Fourier matrix on the declared
one-dimensional mesh and define $\mathsf S_N$ by
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf F_N\mathsf S_N\mathsf F_N^\dagger
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{2\pi iq_0},\ldots,e^{2\pi iq_{N-1}}\right).
\end{equation}
The two translation operators and tensor Fourier transform are
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_x=\mathsf S_N\otimes I_N,
 \qquad \mathsf S_y=I_N\otimes\mathsf S_N,
 \qquad \mathcal F_{2,N}=\mathsf F_N\otimes\mathsf F_N.
\end{equation}
The finite parent in the cell--orbital representation is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat K_{\rho,N}^{(2)}
 =I_{N^2}\otimes T_0
 +\frac\rho2\sum_{\alpha=x,y}
 \left(\mathsf S_\alpha\otimes T_+
 +\mathsf S_\alpha^\dagger\otimes T_-\right).
 \label{eq:kronecker-parent}
\end{equation}
No tensor product of the four-dimensional orbital fibers is taken: Eq.~\eqref{eq:kronecker-parent}
has four orbitals per cell, not sixteen. The Kronecker structure belongs to lattice
translations. Exact unitary conversion gives
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal F_{2,N}\otimes I_4)\widehat K_{\rho,N}^{(2)}
 (\mathcal F_{2,N}^\dagger\otimes I_4)
 =\bigoplus_{i,j}K_\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij}).
 \label{eq:bloch-direct-sum}
\end{equation}
Denote the right-hand side by $\widehat K_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}$. It is the complete Bloch
Hamiltonian, not merely its ordered eigenvalues. For complete unitary eigensystems
\begin{equation}
 K_\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij})
 =Q_{ij}E_{ij}Q_{ij}^\dagger,
 \qquad E_{ij}=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_4),
\end{equation}
the conventional spectral representation is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat E_{\rho,N}^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{i,j}E_{ij},
 \qquad
 \widehat K_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}
 =\mathcal Q_{\rho,N}^{(2)}\widehat E_{\rho,N}^{(2)}
  (\mathcal Q_{\rho,N}^{(2)})^\dagger,
 \qquad \mathcal Q_{\rho,N}^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{i,j}Q_{ij}.
 \label{eq:spectral-representation}
\end{equation}
The diagonal array $\widehat E$ without its eigenvector map $\mathcal Q$ does not identify
an operator in orbital coordinates and may not be subtracted from a tight-binding
Hamiltonian. Equations~\eqref{eq:bloch-direct-sum} and
\eqref{eq:spectral-representation}, their inverses, and the complete spectra are
finite-dimensional construction identities that the producer and independent verifier
must reconstruct.

At each $\mathbf q_{ij}$, a qualified frame
$F_\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij}):\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^4$ defines the block isometry
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V_{\rho,N}^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{i,j}F_\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij})
\end{equation}
and retained Bloch Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}
 =(\mathcal V_{\rho,N}^{(2)})^\dagger
 \left[\bigoplus_{i,j}K_\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij})\right]
 \mathcal V_{\rho,N}^{(2)}.
 \label{eq:retained-direct-sum}
\end{equation}
The retained Wannier/tight-binding representation is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H_{W,\rho,N}^{(2)}
 =(\mathcal F_{2,N}^\dagger\otimes I_2)
 \widehat H_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}
 (\mathcal F_{2,N}\otimes I_2).
 \label{eq:wannier-transform}
\end{equation}
Its $2\times2$ block-circulant entries are precisely the finite Fourier hopping blocks.
Equations~\eqref{eq:retained-direct-sum} and \eqref{eq:wannier-transform} are the
synthetic analogue of subspace selection, gauge choice, and Bloch-to-Wannier conversion.
They introduce no material or first-principles claim.

\section{Aligned Hamiltonian subtraction}

Define the block-diagonal gauge
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{i,j}U_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q_{ij})
\end{equation}
and the comparison Bloch and Wannier operators
\begin{align}
 \widehat A_{B,\rho,a,N}^{(2)}
 &=(\mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)})^\dagger
   \widehat H_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}\mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)},\\
 \widehat A_{W,\rho,a,N}^{(2)}
 &=(\mathcal F_{2,N}^\dagger\otimes I_2)
   \widehat A_{B,\rho,a,N}^{(2)}
   (\mathcal F_{2,N}\otimes I_2).
\end{align}
The covariant subtraction
\begin{equation}
 \widehat A_{B,\rho,a,N}^{(2)}
 -(\mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)})^\dagger
   \widehat H_{B,\rho,N}^{(2)}\mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)}
 =0
 \label{eq:covariant-zero}
\end{equation}
is a construction identity. In Wannier coordinates the corresponding unitary is
generally nonlocal,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal G_{W,a,N}^{(2)}
 =(\mathcal F_{2,N}^\dagger\otimes I_2)
  \mathcal U_{a,N}^{(2)}
 (\mathcal F_{2,N}\otimes I_2),
\end{equation}
and the complete operators satisfy
$\widehat A_W=\mathcal G_W^\dagger\widehat H_W\mathcal G_W$.

The restricted comparison deliberately replaces $\mathcal G_W$ by one cell-local global
rotation $I_{N^2}\otimes R(\phi)$. Only then is the finite operator subtraction
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\widehat H_{W,N}^{(2)}(\phi)
 =\widehat A_{W,\rho,a,N}^{(2)}
 -(I_{N^2}\otimes R(\phi))^\dagger
  \widehat H_{W,\rho,N}^{(2)}
  (I_{N^2}\otimes R(\phi))
 \label{eq:restricted-subtraction}
\end{equation}
scientifically meaningful. Its normalized Frobenius value equals the corresponding
mesh-averaged fiber residual by unitary invariance. Parent-space comparisons likewise
require the projector $\mathcal P=\mathcal V\mathcal V^\dagger$ and an explicit lift:
$\mathcal P\widehat K_B\mathcal P$ may be compared with
$\mathcal V\widehat H_B\mathcal V^\dagger$; a $4N^2$ parent matrix may not be subtracted
directly from a $2N^2$ retained matrix. Every subtraction record must name its source
space, target space, isometry or alignment, basis, gauge, energy reference, units, and
normalization.

\section{Flat-parent analytic comparator}

At $\rho=0$, the protocol fixes the retained frame to the constant two-lowest-eigenvector
frame of $T_0$; the frame algorithm may not inject an additional momentum-dependent
gauge. The retained traceless parent is therefore constant. Uniform integration on $\T^2$
gives
\begin{equation}
 \left\langle e^{2i\theta_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q)}\right\rangle_{\T^2}
 =J_0(a)^2.
\end{equation}
The imaginary part vanishes by global inversion symmetry, so
\begin{equation}
 \mu_{\max}^{(2)}(a)=J_0(a)^2.
 \label{eq:flat-correlation}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 \tau_{o,\min}^{(2)}(a)
 &=\sqrt{\kappa\left[1-J_0(a)^4\right]},\\
 \tau_{o,\mathrm{joint}}^{(2)}(a;0)
 &=\sqrt{2\kappa\left[1-J_0(a)^2\right]}.
\end{align}
The Bessel values are analytic comparators~\cite{dlmf1012}; they never replace a finite
mesh quantity in retained numerical evidence.

Let
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathbf R}=
 \int_{\T^2}e^{-2\pi i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}
 U_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q)^\dagger D U_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q)\,d\mathbf q.
\end{equation}
The dimension-general Parseval argument gives
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{s,\lambda\ge0,\phi}L_{\mathrm{op},\phi}^2
 =\frac{1}{2E_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}
  \sum_{\mathbf R\ne\mathbf0}\norm{B_{\mathbf R}}_F^2.
 \label{eq:flat-locality}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:flat-locality} is a flat-parent theorem. The dispersive calculation
must report its departure rather than assume it.

\section{Square-orbit support and equal-accuracy range}

 r^2(\mathbf R)=R_x^2+R_y^2.
\end{equation}

permutation group and is therefore conjugate closed. Distinct orbit multiplicity depends

 (r_1,r_2)=\left(\max(|R_x|,|R_y|),\min(|R_x|,|R_y|)\right),

\end{equation}
Orbits are ordered by $(r_1^2+r_2^2,r_1,r_2)$. A shell cutoff $S$ retains every orbit
with squared radius at most $S$. The sampled inventory must contain every distinct
support change through the declared cap---equivalently, every attainable squared radius
in canonical order---so no intermediate orbit shell can be skipped when identifying the
first passing support. The exact finite inventory and maximum $S$ must be frozen in
canonical input; the scalar hopping-shell experiment's inventory is a design reference,
not an inherited control.

For a declared sampled shell inventory $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{samp}}$, define

 \widehat S^\star(\rho,a;\tau)=
 \min_{S\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{samp}}}
 \left\{S:\epsilon_{\max}(\rho,a,S)\le\tau E_{\mathrm{ref}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Let $B(S)$ be the number of retained displacement blocks. The primary cost comparison is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\widehat B^\star
 =B\!\left(\widehat S_{\mathrm{comparison}}^\star\right)

\end{equation}
The shell-radius difference is reported secondarily. A missing passing shell produces

\section{Reconstructed-Hamiltonian and spectral error channels}

The maintained 1-D development result now retains maximum/RMS reconstructed-Hamiltonian
and spectral errors for interpolation, deliberate truncation, and total field pairs,
alongside omitted Frobenius power. The definitions below preserve those rank-two
normalizations in 2-D. This does not transfer the 1-D sampled values, verification
status, or evidence identity; the dimension-general owner contracts and the $d=1$
specialization must still be reviewed before a 2-D version freeze.

The subtraction ledger begins before interpolation or truncation. It reports separately:
(i) parent--retained reconstruction after lifting to the parent projector; (ii) covariant
gauge equivalence under the full $\mathcal U$ or $\mathcal G_W$; (iii) restricted global-
rotation residual; (iv) cyclic-to-ordinary conversion power; (v) finite interpolation;
(vi) deliberate truncation; and (vii) total consequences. Channels (i) and (ii) are
construction checks expected to vanish within their frozen numerical tolerances; channel
(iii) is the intended representation residual; the remaining channels are numerical or
deliberate approximation consequences. They are not summed as independent errors.

On a disjoint evaluation mesh, define direct comparison matrices
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rho,a}^{\mathrm{dir}}(\mathbf q)
 =U_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  H_{\rho,N}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathbf q)
  U_a^{(2)}(\mathbf q).
\end{equation}
For any two evaluated Hermitian fields $X$ and $Y$, report the matrix errors
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_{F,\max}(X,Y)
 &=\max_{\mathbf q}\frac{\norm{X(\mathbf q)-Y(\mathbf q)}_F}{\sqrt{2}E_{\mathrm{ref}}},\\
 \mathcal E_{F,\mathrm{rms}}(X,Y)
 &=\left[
 \frac{1}{2|K_{\mathrm{eval}}|E_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}
 \sum_{\mathbf q\in K_{\mathrm{eval}}}
 \norm{X(\mathbf q)-Y(\mathbf q)}_F^2
 \right]^{1/2},
\end{align}
and the separate sorted-band errors
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_{\lambda,\max}(X,Y)
 &=\max_{\mathbf q,n}
 \frac{|\lambda_n[X(\mathbf q)]-\lambda_n[Y(\mathbf q)]|}{E_{\mathrm{ref}}},\\
 \mathcal E_{\lambda,\mathrm{rms}}(X,Y)
 &=\left[
 \frac{1}{2|K_{\mathrm{eval}}|E_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}
 \sum_{\mathbf q,n}
 |\lambda_n[X(\mathbf q)]-\lambda_n[Y(\mathbf q)]|^2
 \right]^{1/2}.
\end{align}
For each reference and comparison shell, both metric pairs are evaluated in three
channels:
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{interp}}&:
 (A_N^{\mathrm{ord}},A^{\mathrm{dir}}),\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{trunc}}&:
 (A_{N,S}^{\mathrm{ord}},A_N^{\mathrm{ord}}),\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{total}}&:
 (A_{N,S}^{\mathrm{ord}},A^{\mathrm{dir}}).
\end{align}
The reference uses its corresponding direct, full, and truncated fields. At each point,
Weyl's inequality supplies the verification relation
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\max_n|\lambda_n(X)-\lambda_n(Y)|}{E_{\mathrm{ref}}}
 \le\frac{\norm{X-Y}_F}{E_{\mathrm{ref}}}
 =\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal E_F(X,Y).
\end{equation}
For deliberate truncation, the squared RMS Hamiltonian error must also reproduce
the normalized ordinary omitted power by Parseval.
The matrix and spectral channels are not interchangeable or additive. Spectral errors
may be much smaller because matrix perturbations can cancel in the eigenvalues. Omitted

\section{Deterministic execution and verification sequence}

Once the open controls below are frozen in a versioned protocol, one 2-D calculation
follows these fifteen steps. The bounded development action exercises the numerical
representation and truncation channels, but it does not satisfy this qualified sequence
or establish that a frozen result or retained package exists.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
 \item Parse canonical input, validate the strict schema, and reject unknown fields,
       nonfinite values, invalid shapes, nonpositive weights, incomplete shell
       inventories, or inconsistent identities.
 \item Assemble the Kronecker parent $\widehat K_{\rho,N}^{(2)}$ and the complete
       fiber direct sum on every declared nested $N\times N$ construction mesh. Verify
       Eq.~\eqref{eq:bloch-direct-sum} without refitting the parent between momenta and
       without materializing a dense $4N^2\times4N^2$ matrix when a matrix-free or
       block-circulant realization suffices.
 \item Compute complete dense Hermitian spectra of every $4\times4$ fiber, select the
       lower rank-two projectors, and apply the external-isolation-gap and projector qualification
       gates. Eigenvalue arrays remain spectral data, not Hamiltonian operands.
 \item Construct the declared global periodic frame using the frozen traversal and
       pairwise Procrustes rules. Apply overlap-rank, reciprocal-sewing, path, and
       plaquette gates; stop if a global frame is not established.
 \item Form $\widehat H_B$, its Wannier/TB representation $\widehat H_W$, and the lifted
       retained operator. Verify frame isometry, projector reconstruction, unitary
       Bloch--Wannier conversion, basis order, and Fourier-block correlation.
 \item Form every $\widehat A_B$ and $\widehat A_W$. Verify covariant equivalence under
       $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal G_W$, then form the separately named restricted
       Hamiltonian subtraction under $I_{N^2}\otimes R(\phi)$. Evaluate the flat
       $J_0(a)^2$ comparator directly and keep it separate from finite-mesh correlations.
 \item Assemble the spectral and restricted-matrix residual quadratics from the original
       aligned matrix elements, including the second-harmonic coefficients and all
       declared weights.
 \item Maximize the continuous harmonic over its half-open fundamental interval, record
       unique or degenerate angle status, and independently reconstruct the optimized
       operator quadratic without using a finite-angle inventory.
 \item Apply all qualification and adjacent-mesh convergence gates, then select and
       record $N_*$ by the prespecified rule without changing thresholds, parameter
       bounds, amplitudes, parent scales, or shell controls. Reaching a cap first gives
       `unresolved'.
 \item Minimize both quadratics over the frozen parameter rectangle and search for a
       joint spectral/operator witness. Verify any witness against the original aligned
       residuals rather than the quadratic summary alone.
 \item If no witness is established, verify the full-ellipse containment premise,
       reconstruct support points, and calculate conservative separation bounds. A
       certified separation requires its lower bound to exceed the frozen resolution.
 \item Assign `compatible', `separated', or `unresolved' using construction data only.
       Later evaluation and truncation diagnostics cannot revise this disposition.
 \item Generate the prespecified shifted, disjoint $M\times M$ evaluation mesh and
       evaluate interpolation and post-decision channels without refitting. The exact
       rule for $M$ and the shift belongs to canonical input.
 \item Compute the finite $N_*^2$ Fourier inventory; isolate the two-line Nyquist union;
       convert to the ordinary lattice; apply every attainable complete square-orbit
       shell through $S_{\max}$; and report conversion, interpolation, deliberate-
       truncation, and total Hamiltonian and spectral channels. Reconstruct every first
       passing sampled shell and retained-block count independently.
 \item Run the independent verifier, validate schemas, bind canonical input, result, and
       verification bytes with SHA-256 digests, and write development records by a
       no-overwrite operation. Creation of an immutable run or content-addressed
       evidence package remains a separate protected action requiring authorization.
\end{enumerate}
