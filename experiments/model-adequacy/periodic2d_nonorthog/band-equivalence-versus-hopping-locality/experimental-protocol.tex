\begin{abstract}
This protocol selects a graphene-inspired synthetic parent on a two-dimensional
triangular Bravais lattice with a two-site honeycomb basis.  It is the first
non-orthogonal extension of the publication-independent experiment \emph{Band
Equivalence versus Hopping Locality}.  A separate matched square--triangular Bravais
control isolates metric and bond-orbit behavior without being identified as graphene.
The inspired parent contains two sublattices and two abstract orbital channels per cell;
it is not a carbon $p_z$ model or a material parametrization.  Complete reference and
rotated rank-two fields are pointwise unitarily equivalent before truncation.  The
primary outcome is the additional metric- and point-group-complete hopping support
needed by the rotated graphene-inspired representation at the same withheld band-error
target.

The triangular bond vectors have a nontrivial integer relation.  Consequently, the
flat-parent global-alignment correlation is a constrained Bessel sum rather than the
square-lattice product of independent $J_0$ factors.  The protocol independently checks
that comparator, the exact residual--non-onsite-weight identity, finite Fourier
conversion, metric-shell generation, and primitive-basis covariance.  Separately
authorized bounded Monkhorst--Pack actions at $N=16,32,64$ now exercise these channels;
they are not an immutable version, material claim, qualified convergence result, or
continuum certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific question and comparison boundary}

For two rank-two Bloch Hamiltonians related by a smooth momentum-dependent rotation,
pointwise eigenvalues agree exactly while Fourier support can differ.  This experiment
asks whether that representation cost persists when the primitive basis is
non-orthogonal and hopping support is defined by physical distance and the lattice point
group rather than by coordinate radius.

The primary target is the graphene-inspired honeycomb-basis parent.  The matched
square--triangular Bravais pair is a separate internal control constructed with the same
onsite and orbital matrices.  Comparisons are conditional on the normalizations below;
they are not universal rankings of lattice geometries.  The experiment measures
representation cost after deliberate truncation, not physical inequivalence, graphene
accuracy, material adequacy, or a preferred Wannier gauge.

The following remain separate channels:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
 \item parent-to-retained reconstruction after an explicit projector lift;
 \item covariant equivalence under the full momentum-dependent rotation;
 \item residual under one cell-local global rotation;
 \item cyclic-to-ordinary finite-Fourier conversion;
 \item off-node interpolation;
 \item deliberate metric-shell truncation; and
 \item total reconstructed-Hamiltonian and spectral consequences.
\end{enumerate}
They are not treated as independent additive errors.

\section{Triangular and matched-square geometry}

The triangular primitive matrix and Gram matrix are
\begin{equation}
 A_{\triangle}=a_0
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&1/2\\
  0&\sqrt3/2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 G_{\triangle}=A_{\triangle}^{\mathsf T}A_{\triangle}
 =a_0^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&1/2\\
  1/2&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:triangular-metric}
\end{equation}
The reciprocal primitive matrix is
$B_{\triangle}=2\pi A_{\triangle}^{-\mathsf T}$.
Reduced coordinates are $\mathbf q\in[-1/2,1/2)^2$, physical wavevectors are
$\mathbf k=B_{\triangle}\mathbf q$, and the Bloch phase of an integer displacement
$\mathbf R$ is $e^{2\pi i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}$.

The positive half of the nearest-neighbor orbit is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_{\triangle,+}
 =\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_2\},
 \qquad m_{\triangle}=3.
 \label{eq:triangular-bonds}
\end{equation}
Together with its negatives it gives six equal-length physical bonds.  The matched
square control uses
\begin{equation}
 A_{\square}=a_0I_2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_{\square,+}=\{\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2\},
 \qquad m_{\square}=2.
\end{equation}

For either geometry $X\in\{\square,\triangle\}$, physical squared distance and integer
point group are
\begin{equation}
 r_X^2(\mathbf R)=\mathbf R^{\mathsf T}G_X\mathbf R,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_X=\{M\in GL(2,\Z):M^{\mathsf T}G_XM=G_X\}.
 \label{eq:metric-and-group}
\end{equation}
For the triangular lattice,
\begin{equation}
 r_{\triangle}^2(\mathbf R)/a_0^2
 =R_1^2+R_1R_2+R_2^2.
 \label{eq:triangular-shell-index}
\end{equation}
Support inventories must contain complete $\Gamma_X$ orbits and every attainable metric
shell through the declared cap.  Coordinate squares $R_1^2+R_2^2$ are not triangular
shell labels.

Reusable lattice, reciprocal-lattice, metric, and finite-domain primitives are owned by
PhysKit.  This experiment owns the selected orbit, basis semantics, shell acceptance,
thresholds, verification obligations, and retained-result policy.

\subsection{Honeycomb basis and graphene-inspired connectivity}

The triangular Bravais lattice becomes honeycomb only after adding two basis sites.  In
reduced direct coordinates, select
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\tau_A=(0,0),
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\tau_B=(1/3,1/3).
 \label{eq:honeycomb-basis}
\end{equation}
The three nearest $A$--$B$ bonds use cell shifts
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{AB}=\{\mathbf0,-\mathbf e_1,-\mathbf e_2\},
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\delta_j=A_{\triangle}
 (\boldsymbol\ell_j+\boldsymbol\tau_B-\boldsymbol\tau_A).
 \label{eq:honeycomb-bonds}
\end{equation}
All three physical vectors have length $a_0/\sqrt3$.  In the periodic orbital convention
the normalized honeycomb structure factor is
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)
 =\frac13\left[1+e^{-2\pi iq_1}+e^{-2\pi iq_2}\right],
 \qquad |\gamma_{\mathrm{hc}}|\le1.
 \label{eq:honeycomb-structure-factor}
\end{equation}
The zeros of this factor give graphene-like internal band contacts.  They do not by
themselves establish a continuum Dirac theory or a carbon band structure.

\section{Matched Bravais-control parents}

Here and below $E_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ is an arbitrary reference-energy normalization, not
a band gap. The common onsite matrix is
\begin{equation}
 T_0=E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1.20&0.06&0&0\\
 0.06&-0.35&0&0\\
 0&0&1.00&0.04\\
 0&0&0.04&1.75
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
To avoid conflating non-orthogonal geometry with an oriented hopping law, use the same
Hermitian orbital coupling in every unoriented bond:
\begin{equation}
 V=E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0.30&0&0.12&0\\
 0&-0.20&0&0.10\\
 0.12&0&0.10&0\\
 0&0.10&0&-0.08
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:orbital-coupling}
\end{equation}
This is the Hermitian sum of the directed block and its adjoint in the orthogonal
predecessor.  It defines a new matched-control parent; numerical results from the
directed-hopping square protocol are not transferred into this comparison.

For $0\le\rho\le1$, define the point-group-invariant structure factor and parent
\begin{align}
 f_X(\mathbf q)
 &=\frac1{m_X}\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
   \cos(2\pi\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf b),\\
 K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)&=T_0+\rho f_X(\mathbf q)V.
 \label{eq:parent}
\end{align}
The normalization fixes $f_X(\mathbf0)=1$ and $|f_X|\le1$ in both geometries.  It does
not make their dispersions identical.

For construction size $N$, let $\mathsf S_N$ be the cyclic shift diagonalized by the
centered unitary Fourier matrix $\mathsf F_N$, and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf S_{\mathbf b}=\mathsf S_N^{b_1}\otimes\mathsf S_N^{b_2}.
\end{equation}
The finite cell--orbital parent on
$\mathcal H_{p,N}^{(2)}=(\mathbb C^N)^{\otimes2}\otimes\mathbb C^4$ is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat K_{\rho,X,N}
 =I_{N^2}\otimes T_0
 +\frac{\rho}{2m_X}\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
  (\mathsf S_{\mathbf b}+\mathsf S_{\mathbf b}^{\dagger})\otimes V.
 \label{eq:finite-parent}
\end{equation}
The tensor Fourier transform must reproduce the direct sum of
$K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q_{ij})$ exactly up to the frozen numerical tolerance.  Four
orbitals occur per cell, not $4^{m_X}$.

At every momentum, complete Hermitian spectra define the lower rank-two projector.  A
qualified periodic orthonormal frame
$F_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q):\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^4$ gives
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)
 =F_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  K_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)F_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:retained}
\end{equation}
A conservative continuous enclosure must establish a positive band-two/band-three gap
for every retained $\rho$ and both geometries.  A sampled minimum is insufficient.  The
frame algorithm must freeze its anchor, traversal, neighbor polar alignment,
reciprocal-boundary sewing, path checks, plaquette checks, and rank thresholds.  Failure
to establish a global periodic frame gives an unresolved case rather than a changed
rank or gauge.

\section{Graphene-inspired dispersive parent}

The primary inspired parent uses the ordered basis
$(L,A),(L,B),(U,A),(U,B)$, where $A/B$ are honeycomb sites and $L/U$ are abstract lower
and upper orbital channels.  Define
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\gamma_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)\\
 \gamma_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)^*&0
 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 K_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\Delta I_2+\rho t_LS_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)&vI_2\\
 vI_2&+\Delta I_2+\rho t_US_{\mathrm{hc}}(\mathbf q)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:graphene-inspired-parent}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=1.20E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad t_L=0.30E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad t_U=0.10E_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \qquad v=0.08E_{\mathrm{ref}}.
\end{equation}
This two-channel construction supplies a rank-two low-energy group with honeycomb
connectivity while keeping two explicit spectator states.  Any implementation has model
role \texttt{TOY}; it is not a \texttt{GrapheneReferenceModel}.  It contains no spin, carbon
$p_z$ radial physics, overlap matrix, fitted graphene hopping, substrate, strain,
spin--orbit coupling, or electron interaction.

Because $\norm{S_{\mathrm{hc}}}_2\le1$, a conservative Weyl bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_2
 \ge 2\Delta-|t_L|-|t_U|-2|v|=1.84E_{\mathrm{ref}}
 \label{eq:graphene-external-isolation-gap}
\end{equation}
for $0\le\rho\le1$.  The calculation must still reconstruct the sampled gap and verify
the bound from the exact input matrices.  Internal contact of the two retained bands is
permitted because the retained object is their rank-two projector, not either individual
band.  A qualified periodic frame defines
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)
 =F_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  K_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)F_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q).
\end{equation}

The finite parent replaces each exponential in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:honeycomb-structure-factor} by its exact cell-shift operator.  Its tensor
Fourier transform, full four-state eigensystem, lower rank-two projector, periodic frame,
projector lift, and Wannier transform obey the same representation checks as the
Bravais-control parents.  At $\rho=0$ its retained pair is scalar, so the nontrivial flat
residual--tail theorem is tested by the separate constant-splitting Bravais control; it
is not falsely attributed to the graphene-inspired endpoint.

\section{RMS-matched gauge family}

Let $g\ge0$ denote the reciprocal-torus RMS rotation amplitude.  For each geometry,
define
\begin{align}
 \theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)
 &=g\sqrt{\frac{2}{m_X}}
   \sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_{X,+}}
   \sin(2\pi\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf b),
 \label{eq:rms-phase}\\
 U_{g,X}(\mathbf q)&=R(\theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)),\\
 A_{\rho,g,X}(\mathbf q)
 &=U_{g,X}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
   H_{\rho,X}(\mathbf q)U_{g,X}(\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:comparison}
\end{align}
For the graphene-inspired parent, use the triangular phase and define
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rho,g,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)
 =U_{g,\triangle}(\mathbf q)^\dagger
  H_{\rho,\mathrm G}(\mathbf q)U_{g,\triangle}(\mathbf q).
 \label{eq:graphene-comparison}
\end{equation}
Distinct positive-half bond characters are orthogonal on $\T^2$, hence
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\T^2}\theta_{g,X}(\mathbf q)^2\,d\mathbf q=g^2.
\end{equation}
Thus square and triangular cases are compared at equal phase RMS rather than equal raw
bond count.  The envelope obeys
$|\theta_{g,X}|\le a_X(g)=g\sqrt{2m_X}$.  The primary range is
\begin{equation}
 0\le g\le g_{\max}^{(2)}=\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt6},
 \label{eq:g-range}
\end{equation}
which ensures $a_X(g)\le\pi/4$ for both cases.  The realized maximum phase is reported
separately and is not silently identified with its triangle-inequality envelope.

The complete fields in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:comparison} and
\eqref{eq:graphene-comparison} have identical pointwise spectra.  Their covariantly
aligned subtractions under the applicable $U_g(\mathbf q)$ vanish.  The restricted
scientific comparison allows only one momentum-independent real rotation $R(\phi)$.
In Wannier coordinates, the full $U_{g,X}$ becomes a generally nonlocal unitary and may
not be substituted into the restricted channel.

\section{Flat-parent analytic comparators}

At $\rho=0$, the retained frame is fixed to the constant lower-two-eigenvector frame of
$T_0$.  Let $D$ be the traceless part of the resulting constant retained Hamiltonian.
For the square control, character independence gives
\begin{equation}
 c_{\square}(g)
 =\int_{\T^2}e^{2i\theta_{g,\square}}d\mathbf q
 =J_0(2g)^2.
 \label{eq:square-comparator}
\end{equation}

For the triangular target, put $x=2g\sqrt{2/3}$ and label the ordered bonds in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:triangular-bonds} by $\mathbf b_1,\mathbf b_2,\mathbf b_3$.  Their
relation $\mathbf b_1-\mathbf b_2-\mathbf b_3=\mathbf0$ constrains the zero Fourier
mode of the Jacobi--Anger product~\cite{dlmf1012}.  With this orientation,
\begin{equation}
 c_{\triangle}(g)
 =\sum_{\ell\in\Z}(-1)^\ell J_\ell(x)^3.
 \label{eq:triangular-comparator}
\end{equation}
The protocol must evaluate this series with a declared symmetric truncation and a
reviewed tail bound, and independently reproduce it by high-accuracy torus quadrature.
Finite-mesh correlations remain separate numerical quantities.

For either $X$, the bond-orbit theorem predicts
\begin{align}
 \inf_{s,\lambda\ge0,\phi}L_{\mathrm{op},X,\phi}^2
 &=\kappa[1-c_X(g)^2]
 =\frac{1}{2E_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}\sum_{\mathbf R\ne\mathbf0}
   \norm{D_{\mathbf R}^{X}}_F^2,
 \label{eq:flat-tail}\\
 \inf_\phi L_{\mathrm{op},X,\phi}^2(0,1)
 &=2\kappa[1-c_X(g)].
\end{align}
The finite calculation must verify the analytic correlation, both residual formulas,
and shell-resolved Fourier weight independently.  No flat identity is assumed for
$\rho>0$.

\section{Finite Fourier conversion and metric-complete truncation}

Construction uses the tensor Monkhorst--Pack mesh in reduced reciprocal coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf q_{ij}=(-1/2+(i+1/2)/N,-1/2+(j+1/2)/N),
 \qquad i,j=0,\ldots,N-1.
\end{equation}
The finite coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathbf R}^{(N)}=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{i,j}
 e^{-2\pi i\mathbf q_{ij}\cdot\mathbf R}H(\mathbf q_{ij}).
\end{equation}
The transform includes the exact Monkhorst--Pack origin phase.  The coefficients
reconstruct the construction nodes but are potentially aliased away from them.  The
cyclic inventory must satisfy the half-step mesh's twisted Nyquist relation.  The
two-line Nyquist union is removed and its power reported before ordinary-lattice
truncation, exactly as in the reviewed square protocol.

A cutoff $S$ retains every displacement satisfying
\begin{equation}
 r_X^2(\mathbf R)/a_0^2\le S
\end{equation}
and therefore every complete $\Gamma_X$ orbit on each attainable shell.  The inventory
must be dense in attainable shell values through $S_{\max}$, conjugate closed, nested,
and frozen before results are inspected.

For the inspired two-site basis, a cell block at $\mathbf R$ contains entries with
basis-aware physical separations
\begin{equation}
 d_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf R)
 =\norm{A_{\triangle}(\mathbf R+\boldsymbol\tau_\beta-
 \boldsymbol\tau_\alpha)}_2,
 \qquad \alpha,\beta\in\{A,B\}.
 \label{eq:basis-aware-distance}
\end{equation}
The primary parity outcome retains complete cell blocks under the Bravais metric.  A
secondary entry-resolved diagnostic reports the distribution of
$d_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf R)$ and must not relabel cell radius as an atomic bond length.

On a disjoint shifted evaluation mesh, define the first passing shell
\begin{equation}
 \widehat S_X^\star(\rho,g;\tau)
 =\min\{S:\mathcal E_{\lambda,\max}^{X}(\rho,g,S)\le\tau\}.
\end{equation}
If $B_X(S)$ counts retained displacement blocks, the within-geometry primary outcome is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\widehat B_X^\star
 =B_X(\widehat S_{X,\mathrm{rot}}^\star)
  -B_X(\widehat S_{X,\mathrm{ref}}^\star).
 \label{eq:block-inflation}
\end{equation}
No passing shell gives an unresolved result, never an extrapolated cutoff.  Cross-
geometry comparisons report both $\Delta\widehat B_X^\star$ and physical shell radii;
block counts and coordinate radii are not interchangeable.

For interpolation, deliberate truncation, and total field pairs, report maximum and RMS
