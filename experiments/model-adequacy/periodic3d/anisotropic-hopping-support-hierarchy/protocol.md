## 2. Scientific question

For one exact finite-support scalar parent on an anisotropic orthorhombic lattice, and
for one nested sequence of explicitly named hopping-support classes, ask:

> What is the smallest tested support class for which one coefficient vector is a
> same-parameter witness for both the frozen spectral and represented-operator
> criteria, and which smaller classes are directly empty or certified separated above
> the frozen resolution?

The answer is hierarchy-relative. It does not establish a physically minimal hopping
range, transfer to another lattice, or validate a material Hamiltonian.

## 3. Exact synthetic parent

### 3.1 State space, lattice, gauge, and units

The parent has one orthonormal, spinless scalar orbital per Bravais cell. Its real scalar
basis is fixed, so no alignment or gauge-selection family is present. Time reversal is
complex conjugation in this basis.

The direct-lattice basis, in nanometres, is

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(1,\frac54,\frac32\right)\ \mathrm{nm},
\qquad
B=2\pi A^{-T},
\qquad
\mathbf k=B\mathbf q,
\quad \mathbf q\in[0,1)^3.
\]

The unequal direct-lattice lengths make axis permutations inapplicable. The declared
point group is the orthorhombic sign-reflection group generated by independent sign
changes of the three integer lattice coordinates. No cubic or diamond symmetry is used.

The energy scale is exactly

\[
E_G=1\ \mathrm{eV}.
\]

The onsite coefficient is fixed to zero, which fixes the finite-mesh and Brillouin-zone
mean energy to zero for the support inventory below. It is not independently refitted by
either channel.

### 3.2 Orbit convention and band

For a canonical nonnegative integer representative
\(\mathbf n=(n_x,n_y,n_z)\), define the sign orbit

\[
O(\mathbf n)=\{(s_x n_x,s_y n_y,s_z n_z):s_j\in\{-1,1\}\},
\]

with duplicate coordinates removed when \(n_j=0\). Axis permutations are never added.
Its multiplicity is

\[
m_{\mathbf n}=2^{|\{j:n_j>0\}|}.
\]

One real coefficient \(t_{\mathbf n}\) is assigned to every block in the orbit. The
orbit function and exact parent band are

\[
\phi_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf q)
 =\sum_{\mathbf R\in O(\mathbf n)}e^{2\pi i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}
 =\prod_{j:n_j>0}2\cos(2\pi n_j q_j),
\qquad
E_\star(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathbf n}t_{\mathbf n}\phi_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf q).
\]

The maximal ordered inventory and exact coefficients are:

| Order | Representative \(\mathbf n\) | Multiplicity | \(t_{\mathbf n}/E_G\) | Support group |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | `(1, 0, 0)` | 2 | `-1/4` | axial nearest |
| 1 | `(0, 1, 0)` | 2 | `-3/16` | axial nearest |
| 2 | `(0, 0, 1)` | 2 | `-1/8` | axial nearest |
| 3 | `(1, 1, 0)` | 4 | `1/32` | face diagonal |
| 4 | `(1, 0, 1)` | 4 | `-3/128` | face diagonal |
| 5 | `(0, 1, 1)` | 4 | `1/64` | face diagonal |
| 6 | `(1, 1, 1)` | 8 | `-1/128` | body diagonal |
| 7 | `(2, 0, 0)` | 2 | `1/64` | axial second neighbour |
| 8 | `(0, 2, 0)` | 2 | `-1/128` | axial second neighbour |
| 9 | `(0, 0, 2)` | 2 | `3/256` | axial second neighbour |

All values are exact binary fractions. The represented block count is
\(\sum_{\mathbf n}m_{\mathbf n}=32\). This analytic parent is experiment-owned and is
not derived from a Kohn--Sham, projected, Wannier, or fitted operator.

## 4. One maximal parameter space and four support classes

Let \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{10}\) use the exact table ordering. Every candidate band is

\[
E_\theta(\mathbf q)=\sum_{j=0}^{9}\theta_j\phi_j(\mathbf q).
\]

Every class remains embedded in this maximal coordinate space; an inactive coefficient
is fixed to zero rather than deleted or renormalized. The four nested classes are:

| Class ID | Active indices | Newly admitted support |
|---|---:|---|
| `axial-nearest` | `0..2` | three independent nearest-axis orbits |
| `face-diagonal` | `0..5` | three independent face-diagonal orbits |
| `body-diagonal` | `0..6` | one body-diagonal orbit |
| `axis-second-neighbour` | `0..9` | three independent second-axis orbits |

These are support groups, not Euclidean-radius shells. Their order is an explicit
experiment design and is not inferred from the anisotropic physical lengths.

The common coefficient domain is

\[
-\frac12 E_G\le\theta_j\le\frac12 E_G
\quad (j=0,\ldots,9).
\]

The exact target lies strictly inside this box. Each class uses the corresponding affine
slice with inactive coordinates fixed to zero.

Only the objective-construction mesh defines channel quadratics, threshold membership,

\[
K_{\mathrm{objective}}=
\left\{\left(\frac{i}{9},\frac{j}{11},\frac{k}{13}\right):
0\le i<9,\ 0\le j<11,\ 0\le k<13\right\}.
\]

It contains 1287 points in deterministic C order with the final coordinate varying
fastest.

The disjoint evaluation-only mesh is

\[
K_{\mathrm{eval}}=
\left\{\left(\frac{i+1/2}{10},\frac{j+1/2}{12},

## 6. Frozen-form channel proposal

### 6.1 Spectral channel

Use the positive coordinate weight

\[
w(\mathbf q)=1+\frac18\cos(2\pi q_x)
                +\frac1{16}\cos(2\pi q_y)
                -\frac1{32}\cos(2\pi q_z).
\]

It obeys \(w\ge25/32>0\). On the declared product mesh its sum is exactly the point
count at the mathematical level. The spectral RMS loss is

\[
L_E(\theta)^2=
\frac{\sum_{\mathbf q\in K_{\mathrm{objective}}}w(\mathbf q)
       [E_\theta(\mathbf q)-E_\star(\mathbf q)]^2}
     {E_G^2\sum_{\mathbf q\in K_{\mathrm{objective}}}w(\mathbf q)}.
\]

The nonuniform fixed weight prevents the spectral and equal-block operator channels from
collapsing into a Parseval-identical problem. It is a benchmark design weight, not a
probability distribution or physical uncertainty model.

### 6.2 Represented-operator channel

Every represented block receives equal weight. In orbit coordinates,

\[
L_H(\theta)^2=
\frac1{32E_G^2}\sum_{j=0}^{9}m_j(\theta_j-t_j)^2.
\]

The sum always spans the complete maximal inventory; omitted target coefficients remain
visible when a smaller class fixes them to zero.

### 6.3 Thresholds under review

The proposed dimensionless RMS thresholds and metric resolution are the exact decimals

\[
\tau_E=0.03697,\qquad
\tau_H=0.006555,\qquad
\rho=0.00005.
\]

These values were selected as a pedagogical **design proposal** after exploratory
inspection of channel minima documented in
[threshold-design.md](threshold-design.md). They are not blind, calibrated physical
tolerances, or retained evidence. Before a confirmatory run, the values and the
exploratory status must be sealed in the immutable experiment version; no later witness,
distance, evaluation, or figure result may tune them.

For class \(k\), the channel sets are

\[
\mathcal A_{E,k}=\{\theta\in\mathcal M_k\cap\mathcal B:
L_E(\theta)\le\tau_E\},
\qquad
\mathcal A_{H,k}=\{\theta\in\mathcal M_k\cap\mathcal B:
L_H(\theta)\le\tau_H\}.
\]

An empty channel remains an explicit channel-empty state and is not assigned a finite
set distance.

## 7. Common metric and decisive evidence

The one metric for every class is normalized complete-block distance,

\[
d(\theta,\eta)=
\left[\frac1{32E_G^2}\sum_{j=0}^{9}m_j
(\theta_j-\eta_j)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\]

Compatibility requires one retained \(\theta_w\) whose two reconstructed RMS losses do
not exceed their thresholds. Two channel-specific feasible points are not a common
witness.

For two nonempty sets, certified separation requires a conservative lower bound
\(\underline\delta_k\le\inf d(\mathcal A_{E,k},\mathcal A_{H,k})\) with
\(\underline\delta_k>\rho\). A feasible pair or local optimizer gives only an upper
bound.

Per-class states follow the shared admissible-set contract:

- spectral channel empty;
- operator channel empty;
- compatible through a common witness;
- certified separated above resolution;
- unresolved nonempty; or
- invalid certificate premise.

A smallest tested compatible class may be reported only when it has a common witness
and every smaller class is directly empty or certified separated. Any unresolved or
invalid smaller class blocks minimality.

## 8. Certificate proposal and premises

In one active-coordinate slice, each nonempty channel quadratic has the form

\[
L_c(\theta)^2=L_{c,\min}^2+
(\theta-\mu_c)^TQ_c(\theta-\mu_c),
\qquad Q_c\succ0.
\]

For a retained nonzero covector \(u\), full-ellipsoid support is

\[
\min u^T\theta=u^T\mu_c-r_c\sqrt{u^TQ_c^{-1}u},
\qquad
\max u^T\theta=u^T\mu_c+r_c\sqrt{u^TQ_c^{-1}u},
\]

where \(r_c^2=\tau_c^2-L_{c,\min}^2\). A positive directional support gap divided by the
metric dual norm gives a conservative distance lower bound.

Full-ellipsoid support is admissible only after the verifier establishes that every
coordinate extremum of that ellipsoid lies inside the active slice of the common box.
If containment fails, box-clipped support is **not** silently substituted; the proposed
certificate is invalid and the class remains unresolved or invalid-premise unless a
separately reviewed method exists.

The producer may use an owner numerical kernel to propose a witness, feasible pair, or
direction. KSD owns containment, metric normalization, resolution, certificate meaning,
and disposition. No generic KSD distance-solver or certificate framework is authorized.

## 9. Independent exact reconstruction

The verifier must not call the producer or use serialized dispositions as an oracle. It
must reconstruct:

1. the lattice, sign orbits, multiplicities, ordering, and support masks;
2. the exact parent coefficients and zero energy reference;
3. the spectral and operator quadratics;
4. channel minima and nonemptiness;
5. witness membership and feasible-pair upper bounds;
6. full-ellipsoid box containment and every retained support gap;
7. the conservative metric lower bound and strict comparison with \(\rho\);
8. hierarchy minimality; and
9. evaluation-only and locality diagnostics without feeding them back into the result.

The objective-construction quadratic has an independent exact-rational reconstruction
route. All
parent coefficients and Fourier coefficients of the weight are rational. The largest
basis frequency is two and products with the weight have frequency magnitude at most
five, below the no-alias limits of the `9 x 11 x 13` product mesh. Discrete Fourier
orthogonality therefore reduces every Gram and target-correlation entry to an exact
rational constant coefficient. The verifier can reconstruct those entries with integer
and rational arithmetic rather than reusing sampled trigonometric rows.

Square roots in support bounds must use conservative rational enclosures or another
reviewed outward-rounding rule recorded in the immutable version. Binary64 agreement,
solver success, or repeated starts alone cannot certify separation.
