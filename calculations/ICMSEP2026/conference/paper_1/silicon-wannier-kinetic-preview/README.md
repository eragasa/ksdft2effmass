## Supported preview statement

For the identified official tutorial source bytes, a rank-four same-frame
construction on a `4 × 4 × 4` reciprocal mesh satisfies the finite represented
identity

\[
H^W(\mathbf{k}) = T^W(\mathbf{k}) + R^W(\mathbf{k})
\]

within the reported floating-point diagnostics. The smooth total-band diagrams
for which the tutorial is familiar assess the spectrum of the total represented
Hamiltonian; they do not by themselves certify separate locality or short-range
compressibility of the represented kinetic and non-kinetic components. The
complete finite transform contains 64 canonical lattice blocks. In the reported
inversion-closed truncation sweep, retaining 57 blocks leaves maximum
ordered-eigenvalue errors of approximately `0.0207 / 0.0843 / 0.0636 eV` for
`H^W / T^W / R^W`. At every nonzero canonical shell radius in this finite
mesh, the maximum kinetic block norm exceeds the maximum non-kinetic-remainder
block norm; the observed ratios range from approximately `1.24` to `2.67`.
The curves are nonmonotone and do not establish a distinct asymptotic decay
rate. Therefore aggressive short-range truncation is not supported for this
one finite representation, but the record does not prove that kinetic blocks
decay more slowly on the infinite lattice.

## Claim limits

This extraction does **not** establish:

- an accepted production silicon parent or Wannier design;
- authenticated production lineage for the external source run;
- reciprocal-mesh, cutoff, window, or pseudopotential convergence;
- infinite-lattice decay or a universal locality bound;
- a validated short-range tight-binding model;
- silicon effective masses, scientific validation, or uncertainty
  quantification; or
- authorization for any new calculator execution.

The source run preserves the official Wannier90 3.1.0 example11 tutorial
numerical settings, including their legacy ultrasoft PBE pseudopotential
reference, 25-Ry wavefunction cutoff, 12 parent bands, four retained Wannier
functions, and `4 × 4 × 4` Wannier mesh. The executed Wannier90 input adds the
two bound output-artifact requests described above. The tutorial settings are
appropriate to the bounded upstream example but differ from this project's
frozen production specifications. The represented remainder `R^W` is a finite
non-kinetic remainder, not an identified continuous scalar potential.
