# Adversarial review: finite-domain geometry and boundary-phase design

## Review boundary

This review challenges the execution-free design, gate audit, protocol, and preflight.
It does not review an implementation or calculated result and does not authorize either.
The review applies devil's-advocate, assumption-busting, and trust-but-verify
perspectives to scientific meaning, represented-space compatibility, case completeness,
verifier independence, and authority containment.

## Findings and corrections

### 1. A stage label would preserve the control-plane ambiguity

**Attack.** Naming the work by an alphabetic stage says nothing about the scientific
capability and encourages the parent Task to remain both roadmap and executable unit.

**Correction.** The active child is named `finite-domain-effects`. Its scope is area,
shape, orientation, and boundary-phase controls. The historical stage terminology
remains only in immutable predecessor records.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 2. The predecessor gate could be satisfied by authored-toy evidence rather than the accepted calculation

**Attack.** A passing fixture suite could be mistaken for the required calculated
model-class evidence.

**Correction.** The gate audit binds the immutable accepted result and verification
identities, confirms one consumed successful attempt, inspects all 208 route records and
1,040 fit records, and requires retained rejected classes and unchanged tolerances.
Authored fixtures are insufficient.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 3. The $12\times12$ geometry could be counted or calculated twice

**Attack.** It appears in both the square-area and area-144 shape lists. Duplicate
calculation could create inconsistent records or inflate the inventory.

**Correction.** One canonical isotropic $12\times12$ evaluation set is referenced by
both analyses. The unique isotropic geometry count is nine, yielding 1,458 evaluations.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 4. Equal matrix dimensions could be mistaken for compatible geometries

**Attack.** Every area-144 rectangle has dimension 144, inviting direct subtraction of
operators acting on different periodic geometries.

**Correction.** Cross-geometry full-matrix subtraction is forbidden. Shape and area
comparisons use canonical radius-two local coefficients and compatible scalar
observables. Geometry metadata remains mandatory even when dimensions agree.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 5. Orientation could silently combine geometry and anisotropic-parent changes

**Attack.** Rotating a rectangle, defect, and anisotropic parent at once could be
reported as one effect and conceal which change caused it.

**Correction.** The design retains two distinct records: a same-parent orientation
contrast with no zero expectation, and an algebraic source-to-swapped-parent covariance
criterion. They may not be pooled.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 6. Boundary-phase analysis could compare gauge-dependent vectors across fibers

**Attack.** Individual eigenvectors at different twists have no direct invariant
identity, and degeneracies permit arbitrary rotations.

**Correction.** Cross-fiber individual-vector comparison is forbidden. The design
reports scalar twist distributions and compares degenerate states only through
projectors within one identified fiber using parent-fixed cluster ranks.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 7. A favorable twist or geometry could be selected after observing results

**Attack.** Reporting only stable twists, monotone sizes, or favorable shapes would
manufacture convergence.

**Correction.** The complete 81-twist mesh is retained for every applicable geometry.
Failed, nonmonotone, no-bound-state, and structured-stop records remain present. No
pooled status or favorable-case filtering is allowed.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 8. Reusing production construction in verification could make agreement tautological

**Attack.** Two call sites using one implementation do not constitute independent
verification.

**Correction.** Production is specified in the centered uniform-link gauge. The
verifier reconstructs through Kronecker seam matrices in the quotient seam gauge and
applies an explicit bridge. It may not import production construction, eigensolution,
observable, reduction, or serialization behavior.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 9. Existing finite-size thresholds could be misrepresented as guaranteed convergence

**Attack.** A single combined pass could hide a failed channel or a nonmonotone
sequence.

**Correction.** Thresholds are reporting criteria only. Area tests only the final
adjacent change; shape reports spread without convergence; same-parent orientation has
no zero expectation; twist reports distributions. There is no pooled pass.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 10. The resource estimate could be treated as execution authority or proactive containment

**Attack.** The inherited 20-minute and 2-GiB estimates are not measured for the exact
2,430-evaluation inventory and do not impose hard limits.

**Correction.** They remain proposed design estimates and post-computation observations
unless a future authorization adds proactive containment. Any later execution question
must restate the exact case count and this limitation.

**Disposition:** corrected with residual implementation-time measurement requirement.

### 11. Finite-domain error could absorb inherited parent or model-reduction error

**Attack.** A global scalar norm could combine parent truncation, finite area, model
reduction, alignment, and eigensolver errors, producing an uninterpretable result.

**Correction.** The design retains nine named error channels and prohibits pooling.
Parent radius-18 truncation remains inherited; model fit residuals remain model
reduction; algebraic eigensolver and serialization diagnostics remain separate.

**Disposition:** corrected.

### 12. The design could drift into the composite and degeneracy study

**Attack.** Projector and principal-angle language could be used to pull the rank-three
composite parent into scope.

**Correction.** This design is rank-one scalar only. Projectors are used solely for
accidental or numerical degeneracies within one scalar finite-domain fiber. Composite
parents, orbital-frame attacks, blind polar alignment, and the composite-degeneracy
follow-on are explicitly excluded.

**Disposition:** corrected.

## Trust-but-verify summary

The design's exact inventory arithmetic is internally consistent:

- $9$ unique isotropic geometries $\times 2$ defects $\times81$ twists = 1,458;
- $2$ orientation pairs $\times2$ defects $\times81$ twists = 324 comparison records;
- $324\times3$ represented operator evaluations = 972; and
- $1,458+972=2,430$ proposed production evaluations.

The predecessor audit agrees with the accepted review: 208 routes, 1,040 retained fits,
17 passing criteria, and independent-verifier `PASS`. No predecessor computation was
rerun.
