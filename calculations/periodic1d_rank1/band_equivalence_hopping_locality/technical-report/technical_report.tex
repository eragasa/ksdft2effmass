\begin{abstract}
A finite-range matrix-valued periodic Hamiltonian need not yield a finite-range scalar
field after spectral reduction to one ordered band.  This report develops that mechanism
as the rank-one control for the periodic-reduction program supporting the scientific
lineage of Ragasa2026a.  The exact parent is a four-state nearest-cell Hermitian field;
the retained scalar Hamiltonian is its lowest ordered eigenvalue.  Because scalar
$U(1)$ conjugation is identically trivial, this family isolates interpolation,
Fourier-tail truncation, direct Hermitian fitting, support/error selection, and numerical
verification without the gauge-dependent represented-operator locality that appears at
rank two.  Exact finite-transform identities, Hermitian coefficient symmetry, error
channel separation, Parseval closure, and the equality of uniform transform and
least-squares routes are stated before numerical evaluation.  Four construction meshes,
a disjoint 1025-point evaluation inventory, sixteen symmetric supports, and two
independent approximation routes provide multiple internal benchmarks.  The sampled
first-to-second gap is $0.37249453282422684\eref$.  Complete-transform interpolation is
near binary64 roundoff for construction sizes $N\ge64$.  Both approximation routes first
satisfy the declared $10^{-8}\eref$ application threshold at range $R=11$ on every
construction mesh, and the passage remains sustained through $R=15$.  At production
size $N=256$, transform and direct-fit coefficient vectors agree within approximately
$4.4\times10^{-16}$--$5.4\times10^{-16}\eref$ over the full range inventory.  An
independent numerical implementation reconstructs the typed result and authenticated
presentation: all 725 named comparisons pass.  These are provisional finite-control
results, not continuum convergence, a global gap certificate, material validation,
publication evidence, or a selected universal hopping range.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Fourier locality; scalar band reduction; hopping truncation;
periodic interpolation; Hermitian least squares; independent verification

\section{Scientific question and evidence boundary}

For the lowest ordered eigenvalue field of an exact finite-range periodic parent, how do
finite interpolation, deliberate Fourier-support truncation, and direct Hermitian scalar
fitting behave as construction resolution and retained hopping range are increased?

The question is deliberately narrower than a material tight-binding calculation.  The
parent is synthetic, one-dimensional, and exactly specified.  The retained object is a
scalar mathematical Hamiltonian on $\C$, not the four-state parent matrix on $\C^4$.
The study compares scalar operators only after their state space, momentum convention,
energy reference, and support convention have been aligned.  It does not invoke Quantum
ESPRESSO, Wannier90, a material crystal, or a continuum differential operator.

The present result was calculated once in memory, reconstructed by an independent
software route, and rendered from a digest-authenticated projection.  No retained
execution directory, sealed package, release, archival deposit, or publication evidence
record was created.  Exact rational inputs and mathematical identities are distinguished
below from sampled binary64 observations.  Passing software checks establish the
implemented finite contract only.

This report is intended to be an upstream technical record for the scalar-control
argument in the Ragasa2026a scientific program.  A paper may distill its theory,
benchmarks, and finite conclusions, but the paper is not the authority for the full
calculation record.  This statement does not modify the repository's current publication
allocation ledger or any exact evidence manifest; those remain separate reviewed
surfaces.

\section{Motivation and literature position}

Localized representations connect reciprocal-space electronic structure to real-space
Hamiltonians.  Maximally localized Wannier functions provide a systematic treatment for
isolated and composite bands\cite{marzari1997,souza2001,marzari2012}, while Wannier
interpolation uses localized real-space information to reconstruct spectral quantities
on dense reciprocal meshes\cite{yates2007}.  The modern software ecosystem makes these
ideas practical across a broad range of material calculations\cite{marrazzo2024}.
Questions of exact compact support and the choice of reduced tight-binding
representations remain distinct from ordinary interpolation
accuracy\cite{sathe2024,ghosh2025}.

This study does not reproduce a maximally localized Wannier function (MLWF) calculation
and does not introduce a localization functional.  Instead, it isolates a smaller
mathematical fact needed before rank-two gauge comparisons can be interpreted: even when
the ambient parent has nearest-cell matrix support, a scalar eigenvalue branch obtained
by diagonalization can have a nontrivial Fourier tail.  Consequently, ``finite-range
parent'' and ``finite-range reduced scalar band'' are different statements.

The numerical structure is also a controlled instance of trigonometric interpolation
and spectral truncation\cite{trefethen2000}.  Complete discrete transforms, deliberately
truncated series, and independently solved least-squares approximations answer different
questions.  Their discrepancies must be separated rather than collapsed into one error
number.  Because results near binary64 roundoff are sensitive to arithmetic and
conditioning, numerical-accuracy claims are interpreted under standard floating-point
cautions\cite{higham2002}.

The rank-one family has a second role.  In a scalar retained space, internal $U(1)$
conjugation cannot alter the represented Hamiltonian.  It is therefore a null control
for the gauge-sensitive matrix locality studied at rank two.  Nontrivial Fourier decay
in this report is caused by spectral reduction and finite approximation, not by an
internal gauge attack.

\section{Exact model and state spaces}

\subsection{Ambient parent and scalar retained operator}

Let the ambient finite representation act on the standard basis of $\C^4$:
\begin{equation}
 K(q)=T_0+e^{2\pi iq}T_{+1}+e^{-2\pi iq}T_{+1}^{\dagger},
 \qquad -\tfrac12\le q<\tfrac12.
 \label{eq:parent}
\end{equation}
The exact coefficient matrices, in units of the positive reference energy
$\eref=1$, are
\begin{equation}
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}
-6/5&3/50&0&0\\
3/50&-7/20&0&0\\
0&0&1&1/25\\
0&0&1/25&7/4
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T_{+1}=\begin{pmatrix}
3/20&0&3/25&0\\
0&-1/10&0&1/10\\
0&0&1/20&0\\
0&0&0&-1/25
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:parent-coefficients}
\end{equation}
Every entry is exact rational data.  Equation~\eqref{eq:parent} is Hermitian for every
real $q$, and its ambient Fourier support is exactly contained in
$\{-1,0,+1\}$.

Write the ordered eigenvalues as
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_0(q)\le\lambda_1(q)\le\lambda_2(q)\le\lambda_3(q).
\end{equation}
The retained scalar Hamiltonian is the mathematical field
\begin{equation}
 H(q)=\lambda_0(q)\in\mathbb{R},
 \label{eq:scalar-band}
\end{equation}
acting on a one-dimensional retained state space.  It is not a $1\times1$ block copied
from $K(q)$, and it is not assumed to inherit the ambient nearest-cell support.  The
eigenvalue definition is backend-independent; a numerical eigensolver only evaluates it
on declared finite point sets.

The first-to-second sampled gap is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(q)=\lambda_1(q)-\lambda_0(q).
\end{equation}
A positive minimum over the declared samples qualifies those samples.  It does not prove
that $\Delta(q)>0$ for every continuum momentum.

\subsection{Why spectral reduction can create a tail}

The characteristic polynomial
$\det(K(q)-\lambda I)=0$ has coefficients that are finite trigonometric polynomials in
$q$, but an ordered root of that polynomial is generally not itself a finite
trigonometric polynomial.  Thus exact finite matrix support before diagonalization does
not imply exact finite scalar support after selecting one eigenvalue branch.  The present
parent was chosen because it preserves compatibility with the rank-two controlled family
while producing a nontrivial scalar tail.  A direct one-orbital nearest-neighbor scalar
parent was rejected as the principal benchmark because every range $R\ge1$ would be
exact and the support study would be nearly trivial.

\subsection{Exact rank-one gauge boundary}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar conjugation invariance]
For any scalar Hermitian field $H(q)\in\mathbb{R}$ and any phase
$U(q)\in U(1)$,
\begin{equation}
 U(q)^{\dagger}H(q)U(q)=H(q).
 \label{eq:u1-invariance}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Scalar multiplication commutes and $U(q)^{\dagger}U(q)=1$, so
$U(q)^{\dagger}H(q)U(q)=H(q)U(q)^{\dagger}U(q)=H(q)$.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:u1-invariance} excludes separate pointwise-phase, global-phase, and
known-inverse-phase locality channels.  Emitting them as if they were distinct physical
calculations would duplicate the same scalar field.  This exact null result is the
structural bridge to the rank-two study: a momentum-dependent internal unitary can
redistribute matrix-valued hopping blocks only when the retained rank exceeds one.

\section{Fourier representation and exact finite identities}

\subsection{Momentum and transform convention}

For an even construction size $N$, use the centered half-open mesh
\begin{equation}
 q_j=-\frac12+\frac{j}{N},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N-1,
\end{equation}
and the centered cyclic representatives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_N=\{-N/2,-N/2+1,\ldots,N/2-1\}.
\end{equation}
For samples $H_j=H(q_j)$, define
\begin{equation}
 t_r^{(N)}=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}H_j e^{-2\pi i q_jr},
 \qquad r\in\mathcal C_N,
 \label{eq:forward-transform}
\end{equation}
and the complete cyclic interpolant
\begin{equation}
 I_N(q)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal C_N}t_r^{(N)}e^{2\pi iqr}.
 \label{eq:complete-interpolant}
\end{equation}
The forward transform carries $1/N$ and the inverse carries no additional factor.

\begin{proposition}[Construction-point closure]
The complete cyclic interpolant satisfies
\begin{equation}
 I_N(q_j)=H_j
\end{equation}
for every construction point, in exact arithmetic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting Equation~\eqref{eq:forward-transform} into
Equation~\eqref{eq:complete-interpolant} yields the discrete character sum
\begin{equation*}
 N^{-1}\sum_{r\in\mathcal C_N}e^{2\pi i(q_j-q_\ell)r},
\end{equation*}
which equals one when $j=\ell$ modulo $N$ and zero otherwise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hermitian scalar coefficients}

Because every $H_j$ is real,
\begin{equation}
 t_{-r}^{(N)}=\overline{t_r^{(N)}}
 \label{eq:hermitian-symmetry}
\end{equation}
for representatives paired away from the cyclic Nyquist class.  Ordinary symmetric
supports are therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_R=\{-R,\ldots,R\},
\end{equation}
with $2R+1<N$ in this protocol.  This strict inequality excludes the ambiguous negative
Nyquist representative from every deliberately truncated model.

The transform-mediated truncation is
\begin{equation}
 T_{N,R}(q)=\sum_{r=-R}^{R}t_r^{(N)}e^{2\pi iqr}.
 \label{eq:truncated-interpolant}
\end{equation}
It is a finite-range scalar hopping model.  Its range is an ordinary lattice range, not
a cyclic distance and not a material length.

\subsection{Separated error channels}

Let $\mathcal E_M$ be the disjoint evaluation inventory.  Define
\begin{align}
 e_{\mathrm{int}}(N)&=\max_{q\in\mathcal E_M}|H(q)-I_N(q)|,\\
 e_{\mathrm{trunc}}(N,R)&=\max_{q\in\mathcal E_M}|I_N(q)-T_{N,R}(q)|,\\
 e_{\mathrm{med}}(N,R)&=\max_{q\in\mathcal E_M}|H(q)-T_{N,R}(q)|.
 \label{eq:error-channels}
\end{align}
The pointwise residual telescopes exactly,
\begin{equation}
 H-T_{N,R}=(H-I_N)+(I_N-T_{N,R}),
\end{equation}
but maxima do not add.  Only the bound
\begin{equation}
 e_{\mathrm{med}}(N,R)\le e_{\mathrm{int}}(N)+e_{\mathrm{trunc}}(N,R)
 \label{eq:triangle}
\end{equation}
is implied.  Threshold decisions use the total mediated error, never the truncation
channel alone.

\subsection{Independent direct Hermitian fit}

The second approximation route solves, with equal positive weights,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{a_0,\{a_r,b_r\}}
 \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \left|H_j-a_0-
 \sum_{r=1}^{R}\left(2a_r\cos 2\pi rq_j-2b_r\sin 2\pi rq_j\right)\right|^2.
 \label{eq:direct-fit}
\end{equation}
The fitted coefficients are $d_0=a_0$,
$d_{+r}=a_r+ib_r$, and $d_{-r}=\overline{d_{+r}}$.  The direct evaluation error is
\begin{equation}
 e_{\mathrm{dir}}(N,R)=
 \max_{q\in\mathcal E_M}|H(q)-D_{N,R}(q)|.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform-route equality]
On the complete uniform construction mesh with equal weights and
$2R+1<N$, the exact least-squares projection in Equation~\eqref{eq:direct-fit} selects
the same retained Fourier coefficients as Equation~\eqref{eq:forward-transform} followed
by symmetric truncation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The constant, cosine, and sine columns are mutually orthogonal discrete characters on
the uniform mesh when their frequencies exclude the cyclic Nyquist class.  Orthogonal
projection onto their span therefore returns the corresponding discrete Fourier
coefficients.  The real Hermitian parameterization is precisely the conjugate-paired
form of those coefficients.
\end{proof}

The implemented direct route nevertheless uses a separate least-squares owner and
control flow.  Its agreement with transform-mediated truncation is therefore both an
analytic expectation and a numerical cross-route benchmark.  For $R\ge1$, the exact
design condition number under the chosen column scaling is $\sqrt2$; the onsite-only
$R=0$ design has condition number one.

\subsection{Parseval and finite perturbation controls}

On the construction mesh, Parseval orthogonality gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}|H_j-T_{N,R}(q_j)|^2
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal C_N\setminus\mathcal S_R}|t_r^{(N)}|^2.
 \label{eq:parseval}
\end{equation}
The square root of the right-hand side is the omitted-coefficient $L^2$ norm retained by
the result.  Equation~\eqref{eq:parseval} is a finite-transform identity, not a theorem
about continuum exponential localization.  Scalar absolute error is also the rank-one
operator norm error, so no separate matrix-to-band perturbation inequality is needed in
this control.

\section{Protocol and benchmark design}

\subsection{Frozen finite inventories}

The construction sizes are
\begin{equation}
 N\in\{32,64,128,256\},
\end{equation}
with $N=256$ designated production.  The evaluation inventory is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde q_i=-\frac12+\frac{i+1/257}{1025},
 \qquad i=0,\ldots,1024.
 \label{eq:evaluation-mesh}
\end{equation}
All coordinates are formed as exact rational numbers before conversion to binary64.
Exact rational set comparison establishes that Equation~\eqref{eq:evaluation-mesh} is
disjoint from every construction mesh.

Every integer range $R=0,\ldots,15$ is evaluated.  The constraint
$2R+1<32$ ensures that every support is an ordinary conjugate-closed support for every
construction mesh.  The sampled gap threshold is $0.25\eref$, the application threshold
is $10^{-8}\eref$, and the final adjacent-mesh refinement tolerance is $10^{-10}$ on the
declared ordered channel vector.

\subsection{Five complementary benchmarks}

The protocol is not a single threshold check.  It contains five complementary
benchmarks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structural null benchmark.}  Proposition 1 proves that scalar internal
  phase rotations cannot alter the represented Hamiltonian.
\item \textbf{Complete-transform closure benchmark.}  The full $N$-coefficient cyclic
  transform must reconstruct construction samples and satisfy Hermitian pairing near
  binary64 roundoff.
\item \textbf{Mesh-refinement benchmark.}  Interpolation and every support-indexed
  channel are compared across $32\to64\to128\to256$.
\item \textbf{Independent-route benchmark.}  Transform-mediated truncation is compared
  with an independently solved Hermitian least-squares model in coefficient and sampled
  spaces.
\item \textbf{Decision benchmark.}  The first jointly passing range, sustained passage,
  support/error nondominance, and near-tie status are reconstructed independently.
\end{enumerate}
These benchmarks separate mathematical structure, transform correctness, discretization
behavior, algorithmic route agreement, and decision policy.

\subsection{Qualification and decision rules}

The sampled gap gate passes only when the minimum across the production construction
mesh and common evaluation inventory is strictly above $0.25\eref$.  The application
decision at range $R$ passes only when both $e_{\mathrm{med}}$ and $e_{\mathrm{dir}}$ are
at or below $10^{-8}\eref$.  The first passage must remain passing at every larger
attained range.  The production range/error mask compares $(2R+1,e_{\mathrm{med}})$;
other diagnostics are not silently substituted as objectives.

No rule in this protocol selects a universal adequate range.  The first-passing result
belongs to this parent, these meshes, this error metric, and this tolerance.

\subsection{Calculation, verification, and rendering routes}

The producer uses PhysKit complex-Hermitian eigensystems, KSD finite-periodic transform
and interpolation owners, and the Project Koios Optimization least-squares owner.  It
returns one immutable typed result containing the parent samples, transform coefficients,
candidates, errors, norms, conditioning diagnostics, decisions, and refinement ledger.

The independent verifier does not invoke the production analyzer.  It reconstructs the
parent matrices and eigensystems, all transforms and fits, Parseval channels, sampled gap,
refinement inventory, masks, passage decision, and visualization projection using direct
NumPy and SciPy formulas.  The verifier owns the exact admitted result bytes and derives
the subject Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit (SHA-256) identity from those bytes.  The
renderer accepts only passing verification and the exact authenticated projection; it
calculates no new scientific values.

The complete production coefficients are conjugate symmetric near numerical precision.
The dominant magnitudes are
$|t_0|=1.2116915623409472\eref$ and

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the finite study demonstrates}

Within the declared inventories, three conclusions are strongly supported.  The reduced
scalar band has a rapidly decreasing but nonzero resolved Fourier tail.  Construction
sizes $N\ge64$ agree on the disjoint evaluation inventory near binary64 roundoff.  Two
mathematically equivalent but separately implemented approximation routes give the same
first-passing support and agree in coefficients and samples near roundoff.

The first-passing value $R=11$ should be read as a controlled decision, not an intrinsic
localization length.  If the application threshold, parent, norm, mesh, or support rule
changes, the selected range may change.  The table is more informative than the single
selection because it preserves the complete finite tradeoff.

\subsection{Why the rank-one result is scientifically useful}

The exact $U(1)$ null result prevents a conceptual category error.  Scalar Fourier
locality can be nontrivial without being gauge-sensitive.  At rank two, by contrast,
pointwise unitary similarity preserves complete spectra while changing the matrix field
whose Fourier coefficients are truncated.  The scalar study therefore identifies which
parts of the later methodology are common numerical infrastructure and which require a
higher-rank state-space action.

\subsection{Why the evidence remains provisional}

The evaluation mesh is dense relative to the construction meshes, but it is still
finite.  No analytic strip, derivative bound, interval enclosure, or arbitrary-precision
study converts sampled gap or error statements into continuum theorems.  The refinement
ladder shows finite stability under its declared channels; it does not quantify model or
roundoff uncertainty.  No external code or retained execution reproduces the result.

\section{Conclusions}

The rank-one controlled-four-state study is a substantive scientific control rather than
a trivial scalar example.  Its exact contribution is to separate three mechanisms:
finite-range ambient matrix structure, spectral reduction to a scalar eigenvalue field,
and finite approximation of that scalar field.  Internal scalar gauge action is exactly
inert, but the reduced band develops a resolved Fourier tail.

The finite calculation then exercises a complete methodology: four construction meshes,
a disjoint evaluation inventory, sixteen ordinary supports, transform and direct-fit

comparisons pass.

This complete record is the level from which publication prose should be distilled.  A
paper may emphasize the scalar null boundary and selected range comparisons, but it
