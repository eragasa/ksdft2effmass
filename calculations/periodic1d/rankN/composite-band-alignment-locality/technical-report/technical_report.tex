\begin{abstract}
Matrix-valued tight-binding representations of an isolated composite subspace are not
unique: a momentum-dependent unitary change of retained basis preserves the represented
spectrum while changing individual Fourier hopping blocks and, consequently, the error
incurred by finite-range truncation. This report develops a controlled periodic
one-dimensional study of that distinction for retained ranks
$r\in\{3,4,8,16\}$ and noncommuting gauge-circuit depths
$L\in\{1,2,4,8\}$. The parent Hamiltonian, isolated retained subspace, and gauge attack
are analytic. Four prescribed retained-space gauge alignments---identity,
global-unitary, pointwise-unitary, and known-inverse-oracle---are compared using a common
closed-loop transport of the analytic reference frame. Odd construction meshes support
finite-periodic matrix Fourier transforms, while a disjoint shifted mesh separates
interpolation error from deliberate finite-support truncation error. The design retains
rank-normalized block tails, Parseval and Hermiticity defects, operator and ordered-
spectrum errors, perturbation bounds, and mandatory mesh-refinement evidence.

Several conclusions follow analytically from the construction: the retained subspace has
an exact uniform exterior-isolation energy $2.10\Eref$; the gauge attack preserves the
retained projector and spectrum; pointwise-unitary and known-inverse-oracle alignment
recover the diagonal reference representation in exact arithmetic; and that recovered
field has Fourier support only for $|R|\leq2$. These are properties by construction, not
new numerical findings. The maintained producer, typed evidence records, bounded
producer-independent reference analysis of analytic-model and attack core invariants,
bounded direct-sum all-gauge Fourier controls, and independent global/pointwise
Procrustes-selection and constant-frame-transport slices are implemented and
software-tested. A complete 16-case producer calculation has also been run locally and
saved as canonical reusable result bytes for the fixed nine-figure inventory. Those
snapshots are visibly marked ``CALCULATED RESULT---UNSEALED'' and are presented as
calculated, integrity-admitted results available for scientific review. This status does
not itself establish scientific validity, seal evidence, or confer publication
acceptance. Their cache integrity and calculation-infrastructure identity are recorded;

requirement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Localized real-space Hamiltonians are routinely obtained by Fourier transforming a
matrix-valued reciprocal-space representation. For an isolated composite band subspace,
the represented eigenvalues are invariant under momentum-dependent unitary rotations of
the retained basis, but the matrix entries and their Fourier coefficients are not. This
familiar gauge freedom underlies both the usefulness and the interpretive difficulty of
Wannier and tight-binding representations
\cite{marzari1997,souzaMarzariVanderbilt2001,marzariEtAl2012}. A short-ranged
representation can be advantageous for interpolation and reduced modeling
\cite{yates2007}, yet spectral equivalence of the untruncated field alone does not imply
that two gauges respond equally to a common finite-range truncation.

The present study isolates this issue from material-specific electronic-structure
uncertainties. It does not run Quantum ESPRESSO, Wannier90, or a spread minimization. It
instead supplies an analytic, finite-dimensional parent Hamiltonian with an exactly
isolated retained subspace and then applies a deterministic higher-rank gauge circuit.
The attacked representation has the same pointwise spectrum and projector as the
reference representation. Any difference in its hopping locality is therefore a
representation effect rather than a change of the underlying retained operator.

The central scientific question is:

\begin{quote}
For a fixed isolated retained operator, how do retained rank, noncommuting gauge-circuit
depth, and a predeclared gauge-alignment family affect matrix-valued hopping
locality and the error of deliberate finite-support truncation?
\end{quote}

This question is intentionally narrower than the problem of finding optimally localized
Wannier functions. The study compares four declared feasible alignment families rather
than searching an unrestricted localization functional. In particular, the analytic
known inverse is retained only as an oracle control. It cannot support a claim about
practical recovery from unknown data.

\section{Background and scientific setting}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Represented operators and gauge covariance}

Let $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{amb}}$ be a finite ambient state space and let
$F(k):\mathbb C^r\rightarrow\mathcal H_{\mathrm{amb}}$ be an orthonormal frame for a
rank-$r$ retained subspace at reciprocal coordinate $k$. For an ambient Hermitian
operator $H_p(k)$, the represented retained operator is
\begin{equation}
 H_F(k)=F(k)^\dagger H_p(k)F(k).
 \label{eq:represented-operator}
\end{equation}
A retained-coordinate gauge transformation acts from the right,
$F(k)\mapsto F(k)U(k)$ with $U(k)\in U(r)$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
 H_F(k)\mapsto H_{FU}(k)=U(k)^\dagger H_F(k)U(k).
 \label{eq:gauge-covariance}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:gauge-covariance} preserves the unordered pointwise spectrum and the
ambient projector $P(k)=F(k)F(k)^\dagger$. It does not preserve individual matrix
entries or Fourier coefficients.

This distinction is fundamental. A physical model is identified by its state spaces,
operator, geometry, units, and energy reference. A finite matrix is a representation of
that operator in a declared basis. Comparing matrices without first aligning their state
spaces and basis conventions can confound a change of representation with a change of
model.

\subsection{Alignment versus localization}

The orthogonal or unitary Procrustes problem selects the unitary factor that best aligns
two frame matrices in a Frobenius norm \cite{schoenemann1966}. Its polar factor can be
computed from an SVD or polar decomposition
\cite{higham1986,golubVanLoan2013}. Pointwise Procrustes alignment supplies one unitary
per reciprocal point. A global-unitary restriction instead supplies one matrix for the
entire reciprocal route. These are different feasible sets, not different numerical
accuracies for the same optimization problem.

Neither alignment is equivalent to minimizing a Wannier spread functional. The present
protocol therefore does not claim to reproduce maximally localized Wannier functions.
Its purpose is diagnostic: it asks how much locality is lost under a known gauge attack
and how much is recovered under precisely declared alignment information.

\subsection{Fourier locality and finite support}

For a periodic represented field $H(k)$, continuum hopping blocks are
\begin{equation}
 T(R)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-ikR}H(k)\,dk,
 \qquad R\in\mathbb Z.
 \label{eq:continuum-fourier}
\end{equation}
Rapid decay of $\|T(R)\|$ supports accurate finite-range approximation. Analytic
periodicity often implies rapid Fourier decay, but a nonlinear momentum-dependent gauge
rotation can generate an infinite harmonic inventory even when the reference field has
finite support. Thus ``same bands'' and ``same truncation behavior'' are distinct
statements.

A sampled finite transform introduces two additional distinctions. First, discrete
coefficients are a finite representation of sampled data and may contain aliasing from
unresolved continuum harmonics. Second, deliberately setting blocks outside a chosen
support to zero is a model-reduction operation. The protocol keeps continuum behavior,
finite-mesh interpolation, and deliberate truncation separate.

\subsection{A gentle methods primer}
\label{sec:methods-primer}

A frame is a matrix whose columns are an orthonormal basis for the retained subspace.
The subspace fixes the projector, but it does not fix those columns: if $A(k)$ is a frame
and $Q(k)$ is unitary, then $A(k)Q(k)$ spans the same subspace. This is the frame, or
gauge, ambiguity. The multiplication is on the right because $Q(k)$ mixes retained
columns; left multiplication would instead act in the ambient state space. A represented
operator consequently changes by unitary conjugation, even though its spectrum and
ambient projector do not.

To align a candidate frame $A(k)$ to a reference frame $B(k)$, the right-action
Procrustes problem minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \|A(k)Q(k)-B(k)\|_\Frob^2
 =\|A(k)\|_\Frob^2+\|B(k)\|_\Frob^2
 -2\,\operatorname{Re}\Tr\!\left(Q(k)^*A(k)^*B(k)\right).
 \label{eq:primer-procrustes}
\end{equation}
This expansion explains why the cross matrix is $A(k)^*B(k)$: it is the matrix paired
with the right-acting $Q(k)^*$ in the trace. The reversed product corresponds to a
different orientation, and products in the ambient space solve a different problem. In
the notation used below, $A=C$ is the attacked candidate and $B=R$ is the reference.
When $A(k)^*B(k)=Q(k)P(k)$ is an exact polar factorization with $P(k)$ positive
semidefinite, $Q(k)$ minimizes the local objective; positive definiteness supplies the
usual uniqueness condition.

The four alignment fields differ by what may vary with $k$. Identity alignment fixes
$Q(k)=I$. Global alignment permits one constant $Q$ for every point. Local, or
pointwise, alignment permits a separately selected $Q(k)$ at each point. The known
inverse is a special supplied local field satisfying $A(k)Q(k)=B(k)$ exactly in the
analytic control; it is not information available in an ordinary inverse problem.
These are nested information restrictions, not four error definitions.

Fourier locality begins only after a represented field and a gauge have been fixed. On a
finite periodic mesh the transform is exact finite algebra for the sampled field. A
single constant unitary conjugation passes through coefficient extraction,
reconstruction, and support truncation. A momentum-dependent unitary generally does not:
it can preserve every pointwise eigenvalue and Frobenius norm while redistributing weight
among Fourier modes. Thus finite-mesh coefficient identities do not by themselves prove
continuum Fourier decay, absence of aliasing, or convergence as the mesh is refined.

Finally, the report keeps the residual ledger explicit. Interpolation compares the full
finite reconstruction with the direct field; deliberate truncation compares the
support-truncated and full reconstructions; total error compares the truncated field with
the direct field. Their fields add with a fixed sign convention, but their scalar maxima
or RMS values need only satisfy norm inequalities. Gauge choice, finite Fourier
representation, and these error channels are therefore related stages, not interchangeable
notions of ``alignment error.''

\section{Analytic model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Geometry, state spaces, and units}

The selected one-dimensional specialization uses the dimensionless unit lattice and reduced
Brillouin torus $\Torus=[-\pi,\pi)$. For retained rank $r\geq3$, the ambient state space
is $\mathbb C^{2r}$ with the retained coordinates ordered before the exterior
coordinates. The canonical embedding $E_r:\mathbb C^r\rightarrow\mathbb C^{2r}$ and
projector are
\begin{equation}
 E_r=\begin{pmatrix}I_r\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad P_0=E_rE_r^\dagger.
\end{equation}
All energies are expressed in a declared dimensionless unit with positive reference
scale $\Eref=1$ during numerical evaluation. Formulas retain $\Eref$ to expose the
normalization.

The constant-projector control uses the analytic frame $F_r(k)=E_r$ and identity ambient sewing.
The state-space map is therefore known directly rather than inferred from eigenvectors.
Internal crossings among retained energies are permitted because the complete retained
projector, not a sequence of individually labeled bands, is the scientific object.

\subsection{Rank-normalized parent spectrum}

For retained coordinate $a\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 x_{r,a}=\frac{2a-r-1}{r-1}\in[-1,1],
 \qquad
 c(k)=\frac34\cos k+\frac14\cos 2k.
\end{equation}
The retained and exterior energies are
\begin{align}
 \frac{\epsilon_a(k)}{\Eref}
 &=0.40x_{r,a}+0.25\left(1+0.20x_{r,a}\right)c(k),
 \label{eq:retained-energy}\\
 \frac{\epsilon_a^{\mathrm{ext}}(k)}{\Eref}
 &=3.00+0.30x_{r,a}+0.10c(k).
 \label{eq:exterior-energy}
\end{align}
Let $D_r(k)=\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon_1(k),\ldots,\epsilon_r(k))$ and define the
ambient parent
\begin{equation}
 H_p^{(r)}(k)=
 \begin{pmatrix}D_r(k)&0\\0&D_{\mathrm{ext}}(k)\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:parent-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The represented reference operator is exactly $F_r^\dagger H_p^{(r)}F_r=D_r$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact exterior isolation]
For every selected finite rank and every $k\in\Torus$, the exterior spectrum lies at
least $2.10\Eref$ above the retained spectrum. The lower bound is attained at $k=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $k$, the retained maximum occurs at $x=1$ and the exterior minimum at $x=-1$.
Using Equations~\eqref{eq:retained-energy}--\eqref{eq:exterior-energy}, their separation
is $(2.30-0.20c(k))\Eref$. Since $|c(k)|\leq1$, the separation is at least
$2.10\Eref$, and $c(0)=1$ gives equality.
\end{proof}

The proposition is an analytic property of the finite model. A sampled minimum is
retained separately as a numerical reconstruction check and is never relabeled as the
continuum certificate.

\subsection{Deterministic noncommuting gauge attack}

Adjacent retained coordinates are mixed by real Givens rotations. For layer $\ell$ and
pair start $j$, define
\begin{equation}
 y_{r,j}=\frac{2j-r}{r-1},
 \qquad
 s_\ell(k)=\frac34\sin k+\frac14(-1)^\ell\sin 2k,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{\ell j}(k)=0.35(-1)^{\ell+j}
 \left(1+0.15y_{r,j}\right)s_\ell(k).
 \label{eq:attack-angle}
\end{equation}
Layer $\ell$ acts on the disjoint adjacent pairs with $j\equiv\ell\pmod2$. Factors
within one layer commute; neighboring layers generally do not. Increasing layer order is
part of the definition. The depth-$L$ product is denoted $G_{r,L}(k)\in SO(r)$.

The attacked frame and represented operator are
\begin{equation}
 F'_{r,L}(k)=F_r(k)G_{r,L}(k),
 \qquad
 H'_{r,L}(k)=G_{r,L}(k)^\dagger D_r(k)G_{r,L}(k).
 \label{eq:attacked-fields}
\end{equation}
One layer is an incomplete-connectivity baseline; two or more alternating layers cover
every adjacent edge and form a connected chain. Increasing depth changes the
representation while leaving the retained operator and projector invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Exact covariant equivalence]
For every $r$, $L$, and $k$, the attacked frame is orthonormal, its ambient projector is
$P_0$, and $H'_{r,L}(k)$ has the same unordered spectrum as $D_r(k)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each Givens factor is orthogonal, hence $G_{r,L}^\dagger G_{r,L}=I_r$. Substitution into
Equation~\eqref{eq:attacked-fields} proves frame orthonormality and projector equality.
The represented operators are related by a unitary similarity, which proves spectral
equivalence.
\end{proof}

The nonlinear entries of $G_{r,L}$ generally contain harmonics beyond those present in
the angle functions. Consequently the attacked represented field is analytic and
periodic but is not assigned a finite Fourier support by construction.

\section{Gauge-alignment methodology}
\label{sec:alignment}

\subsection{Canonical source and target orientation}

Let $R(k)=F_r(k)$ be the reference, $C(k)=F'_{r,L}(k)$ the attacked candidate, and
$H_C(k)=H'_{r,L}(k)$ the represented candidate operator. Every prescribed gauge
alignment acts from the right:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_U(k)=C(k)U(k),
 \qquad
 \widehat H_U(k)=U(k)^\dagger H_C(k)U(k).
 \label{eq:alignment-application}
\end{equation}
Returning $U^\dagger$ while retaining Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-application} would be
a convention error, not an equivalent implementation.

For cross-covariance $X=C^\dagger R$ with complete SVD
$X=L\Sigma V^\dagger$, the admitted unitary polar factor is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P(X)=LV^\dagger.
 \label{eq:polar-factor}
\end{equation}
It is selected only when
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\min}(X)>\tau_{\mathrm{sv}}(X),
 \qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{sv}}(X)=\max(10^{-12},10^{-10}\sigma_{\max}(X)).
 \label{eq:rank-gate}
\end{equation}
Equality is deficient. No arbitrary null-space completion is introduced.

\subsection{Four prescribed gauge alignments}

\paragraph{Identity, wire value \texttt{identity}.}
$U_{\mathrm{id}}(k)=I_r$. This control measures the attacked representation as supplied.

\paragraph{Global-unitary, wire value \texttt{global-unitary}.}
One matrix minimizes the equal-weight objective
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathcal S}(U)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|r}
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal S}\|C(k)U-R(k)\|_\Frob^2.
 \label{eq:global-objective}
\end{equation}
The selection sample is exactly the final construction mesh $\mathcal K_{2049}$. The
selected unitary is reused on the coarser construction meshes and shifted evaluation
mesh without refitting.

\paragraph{Pointwise-unitary, wire value \texttt{pointwise-unitary}.}
At every construction point,
$U_{\mathrm{pt}}(k)=\mathcal P(C(k)^\dagger R(k))$. Selection is repeated separately on
each construction mesh. Selected pointwise unitaries are not interpolated to the shifted
evaluation mesh; the direct recovered reference there is the analytic $D_r(k)$.

\paragraph{Known-inverse-oracle, wire value \texttt{known-inverse-oracle}.}
$U_{\mathrm{oracle}}(k)=G_{r,L}(k)^{-1}=G_{r,L}(k)^\dagger$. This action performs no
optimization. It checks construction, orientation, application, and Fourier conventions.
It is not a practical estimator.

The four names identify gauge alignments, not diagnostic channels. ``Error channel'' is
reserved for the later interpolation, truncation, and total residual decomposition.

\subsection{Alignment integrity evidence}

For every selected Procrustes factor, the producer retains all singular values, the
applied cutoff, strict rank margin, condition number when defined, and the objective
recomputed from the original frames. It also evaluates
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{unit}}=\|U^\dagger U-I_r\|_\Frob,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}
 =\frac{\|U^\dagger X-X^\dagger U\|_\Frob}{\sqrt r},
\end{equation}
plus the covariance-objective identity and the negative part of the Hermitian polar
factor. These checks distinguish a nominally returned matrix from a scientifically
admitted alignment. The bounded independent selection reference reconstructs
$X_{\mathrm{global}}=2049^{-1}\sum_jG(q_j)^\dagger$ and evaluates its right polar factor
as $X_{\mathrm{global}}(X_{\mathrm{global}}^\dagger
X_{\mathrm{global}})^{-1/2}$ through an Hermitian eigendecomposition rather than the
producer SVD solver. It uses $U_j=G(q_j)^\dagger$ directly for the pointwise controls and
recomputes the retained rank, residual, integrity, and selected-unitary evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise and oracle recovery]
For the constant-projector construction, pointwise-unitary and known-inverse-oracle alignment both
recover $F_r(k)$ and $D_r(k)$ in exact arithmetic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $C(k)=F_rG_{r,L}$ and $R(k)=F_r$, so
$C(k)^\dagger R(k)=G_{r,L}(k)^\dagger$. This matrix is unitary and has all singular
values equal to one. Its unique full polar factor is itself, which equals the known
inverse. Substitution into Equation~\eqref{eq:alignment-application} gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The two actions remain separate in software because one reconstructs a factor from
sampled frames while the other receives the generating inverse. Their agreement tests an
implementation contract; it is not an empirical discovery.

\section{Common closed-loop frame transport}
\label{sec:transport}

Transport is applied to the analytic reference frame and is common to all four gauge
alignments. It is not a fifth alignment and is not appended only to the pointwise case.
This separation prevents transport defects from being misattributed to gauge-selection
quality.

On the ordered construction mesh, let $Y_n=F_r(k_n)$ and set
$\widetilde F_0=Y_0$. At each neighbor link, form
\begin{equation}
 O_n=\widetilde F_n^\dagger Y_{n+1}=L_n\Sigma_nR_n^\dagger,
 \qquad
 Q_n=R_nL_n^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \widetilde F_{n+1}=Y_{n+1}Q_n.
 \label{eq:parallel-transport}
\end{equation}
Then $\widetilde F_n^\dagger\widetilde F_{n+1}$ is Hermitian positive semidefinite. The
strict rank rule in Equation~\eqref{eq:rank-gate} applies to every link. A deficient link
terminates dependent transport; later links are unattempted rather than fabricated.

After traversing the period, write the endpoint mismatch as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H=Z\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\gamma_1},\ldots,e^{i\gamma_r})Z^\dagger,
 \qquad \gamma_a\in(-\pi,\pi].
 \label{eq:holonomy}
\end{equation}
The branch choice is part of the method. It assigns $+\pi$ to eigenvalue $-1$ and thereby
fixes the distributed root. Closed frames are
\begin{equation}
 F_n^{\mathrm{closed}}=\widetilde F_n
 Z\operatorname{diag}(e^{-in\gamma_1/M},\ldots,e^{-in\gamma_r/M})Z^\dagger.
 \label{eq:distributed-closure}
\end{equation}
If $\widetilde F_M=\widetilde F_0\mathcal H$, then
$F_M^{\mathrm{closed}}=F_0^{\mathrm{closed}}$. For the constant reference frame and
identity sewing, the exact model predicts trivial transport and zero endpoint phases;
the numerical transport remains valuable as an action-orientation and closure control.
The bounded independent transport reference instantiates that exact prediction directly:
every overlap has singular spectrum $(1,\ldots,1)$, every closure phase is zero, and all
three retained closure right actions are $I_r$. It compares the link and closure evidence
without invoking the transporter.

The phase tuple is an unordered multiset scientifically. Deterministic sorting is a wire
convention, not a continuation of individual band labels through degeneracies.

\section{Finite-periodic Fourier and locality methodology}
\label{sec:fourier}

\subsection{Construction and evaluation meshes}

The three construction meshes are
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_M=\left\{-\pi+\frac{2\pi n}{M}:n=0,\ldots,M-1\right\},
 \qquad M\in\{513,1025,2049\}.
 \label{eq:construction-mesh}
\end{equation}
All are odd, centered, and half open. The direct evaluation mesh is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_{\mathrm{eval}}=
 \left\{-\pi+\frac{2\pi(m+1/2)}{8191}:m=0,\ldots,8190\right\}.
 \label{eq:evaluation-mesh}
\end{equation}
It is disjoint from every construction mesh and cannot influence a fit, support,
tolerance, or reporting rule.

For odd $M$, the canonical translation inventory is
$\mathcal R_M=\{-(M-1)/2,\ldots,(M-1)/2\}$. There is no self-inverse Nyquist singleton;
the canonical disposition is \texttt{absent-on-odd-mesh}.

\subsection{Discrete transform, diagnostic ranges, and support policy}

For one gauge alignment $a$, the finite-periodic transform is
\begin{equation}
 T_{a,M}(R)=\frac1M\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_M}e^{-ikR}H_a(k),
 \qquad
 H_{a,M}(k)=\sum_{R\in\mathcal R_M}e^{ikR}T_{a,M}(R).
 \label{eq:discrete-transform}
\end{equation}
Only the forward transform carries $1/M$. The complete coefficient inventory is used for
reconstruction and tail calculations. The public result retains the bounded norm
inventory $|R|\leq128$ rather than serializing all dense coefficient arrays.

The declared integer ranges serve two scientifically distinct roles. In the
hopping-diagnostic inventory, a range partitions the unchanged coefficient field into
included and omitted blocks for measurement only. In the retained-support inventory,
the same integer interval defines a new field by setting exterior coefficients to zero.
Numerically identical ranges therefore remain non-interchangeable: the first operation
is diagnostic, whereas the second is an irreversible model-reduction action.

The declared maximum ranges are
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_S=\{R\in\mathbb Z:|R|\leq S\},
 \qquad S\in\{0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}.
 \label{eq:supports}
\end{equation}
Every range contains inversion pairs. All nine ranges are evaluated in both typed
inventories; no first-passing or plot-selected range is promoted.

For the reference diagonal field, Equations~\eqref{eq:retained-energy} and
\eqref{eq:continuum-fourier} imply an exact support certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Reference support]
The continuum Fourier coefficients of $D_r(k)$ vanish for every $|R|>2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every diagonal entry of $D_r$ is a linear combination of $1$, $\cos k$, and
$\cos2k$. These harmonics have Fourier indices only at $0$, $\pm1$, and $\pm2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
In exact arithmetic, pointwise-unitary and known-inverse-oracle alignment recover a
represented field with continuum support $|R|\leq2$.
\end{corollary}

This is an analytic certificate. Finite-transform reconstruction and binary64 residuals
still require numerical checks.

\subsection{Tail and error diagnostics}

For hopping-diagnostic cutoff $S$, define the omitted quadratic weight, its square root,
and the block-$\ell^1$ tail from the unchanged coefficients:
\begin{align}
 W_{a,M}(S)&=\sum_{R\notin\Omega_S}\|T_{a,M}(R)\|_\Frob^2,\\
 N_{a,M}(S)&=\sqrt{W_{a,M}(S)},\\
 L_{a,M}(S)&=\sum_{R\notin\Omega_S}\|T_{a,M}(R)\|_\Frob.
\end{align}
Cross-rank comparisons retain $W/(r\Eref^2)$ and $N/(\sqrt r\Eref)$ in addition to raw
values. This predeclared normalization prevents matrix dimension alone from determining
a trend.

For retained support $\Omega_S$, the shifted-mesh full interpolant, truncated
interpolant, and direct analytic reference define
\begin{align}
 e_{\mathrm{interp}}(k)&=H_{a,M}(k)-H_a^{\mathrm{direct}}(k),\\
 e_{\mathrm{trunc}}^{(S)}(k)&=H_{a,M}^{(S)}(k)-H_{a,M}(k),\\
 e_{\mathrm{total}}^{(S)}(k)&=H_{a,M}^{(S)}(k)-H_a^{\mathrm{direct}}(k).
 \label{eq:error-channels}
\end{align}
The pointwise identity
$e_{\mathrm{total}}=e_{\mathrm{interp}}+e_{\mathrm{trunc}}$ is checked directly.
Scalar maxima of the three error channels are not added: their maximizing points can
differ, and cancellation in the total error is reported rather than called convergence.

For each residual, the study records maximum and RMS Frobenius operator error and maximum
and RMS ordered-spectrum error. Ordered spectra are compared as sorted multisets rather
than by tracking eigenvectors through crossings. At every point, the finite-dimensional
bounds
\begin{equation}
 \max_i|\lambda_i(H_{\mathrm{approx}})-\lambda_i(H_{\mathrm{reference}})|
 \leq\|e\|_2\leq\|e\|_\Frob
 \label{eq:perturbation-bound}
\end{equation}
are checked \cite{hornJohnson2013}. Qualified saturation ratios are reported only when
their denominators exceed the frozen floor.

\subsection{Refinement and qualification}

The smaller meshes are mandatory refinement controls. Blockwise comparisons cover both
adjacent mesh pairs. The final scalar inventory uses the mandatory
$1025\rightarrow2049$ pair and the absolute-relative rule
\begin{equation}
 |q_f-q_c|\leq\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}+\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}
 \max(|q_c|,|q_f|),
 \label{eq:refinement-rule}
\end{equation}
with a denominator floor for the separately reported relative change. The shared
\path{ScalarRefinementAnalyzer} owns this arithmetic; the higher-rank diagnostic
inventory owner defines which scientific quantities enter the ledger.

A case is numerically qualified only if its mandatory construction, model, transport,
alignment, Fourier, interpolation, and refinement gates pass. A global-unitary
aggregate may instead carry the bounded disposition
\texttt{aggregate-rank-deficient}; this makes only global-unitary downstream values
inapplicable. Pointwise rank deficiency, documented backend failure, alignment integrity
failure, or oracle integrity failure invalidates the complete case.

\subsection{Reasonable accuracy scales and computational precision}

A numerical tolerance is an acceptance standard, not a prediction that every residual
will stop at that value. For normalized binary64 calculations near unit scale, machine
epsilon is approximately $2.22\times10^{-16}$. Matrix products, Fourier sums over up to
2049 points, eigensolvers, and repeated factorizations normally accumulate more error
than one machine epsilon. Consequently, residuals of order $10^{-12}$ are a reasonable
strict standard for structural identities in this calculation; $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-10}$
are reasonable standards for Fourier, spectrum, transport, and recovery comparisons;
and the complete-interpolant operator gate is deliberately looser at $10^{-9}$. The
scientific effect-resolution scale is $10^{-6}$, several orders of magnitude above all
of these numerical gates. Table~\ref{tab:tolerances} gives the diagnostic-specific
values and normalizations.

Some present controls nevertheless produce measured residuals of order $10^{-16}$, and
one decomposition defect is smaller still. Those values arise where an analytic identity,
finite support, or nearly exact cancellation is represented especially faithfully in
binary64 arithmetic. They show that the measured residual reached the roundoff scale;
they do not establish 16 or more reliable decimal digits, justify a $10^{-18}$ acceptance
tolerance, or improve the scientific resolution below $10^{-6}$. Exact floating-point
zero is reported separately because it is a representational outcome, not evidence of
infinite accuracy. In logarithmic figures, $10^{-18}$ is only an axis-placement device
that keeps exact zeros and exceptionally small positive residuals visible; all values at
that scale are interpreted as computational-precision or roundoff-scale results.

\subsection{Claim-resolution rules}

The protocol does not infer a locality effect from visual separation alone. For a
qualified case, gauge-induced locality extension at $S=2$ is resolved only if the
identity tail is at least the frozen $10^{-6}$ scale, the oracle tail is at most
$10^{-10}$, and the identity total ordered-spectrum maximum error after $S=2$
truncation is at least $10^{-6}E_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Rank or depth differences are descriptive unless their normalized difference
exceeds both $10^{-6}$ and ten times the larger observed refinement change. These rules
are fixed before execution and do not assert that an effect will be found.

No monotonic trend with rank or depth is assumed. Noncommuting products can produce
cancellation, and a finite ladder cannot establish an asymptotic law.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the controlled comparison can establish}

The construction isolates representation dependence. Since the parent, projector, and
untruncated retained spectrum are held fixed, a resolved difference in finite-support
error would demonstrate that short-range compressibility depends on retained-space gauge
within this finite analytic family. It would not demonstrate that the parent model is
physically realistic or that one gauge is universally optimal.

Pointwise-unitary and oracle recovery form a strong implementation control because their
exact target is known. Global-unitary alignment is scientifically different: one matrix
cannot generally undo a momentum-dependent noncommuting attack. A nonzero qualified
global-frame residual is a supporting measure of the limitation of that restricted
feasible set, not solver failure. Only the separately frozen maximum normalized
represented-operator residual resolves the protocol's global-alignment claim, and that
operand is not retained by the current public result. Conversely, a small residual of
either declared kind at one finite case would not imply equivalence of global and
pointwise alignment in general.

\subsection{Interpreting rank and depth}

Retained rank changes matrix dimension and the number of adjacent mixing edges. Circuit
depth changes the ordered product of generally noncommuting rotations. The selected
$\sqrt r$ and $r$ normalizations make cross-rank magnitudes interpretable without
removing all rank dependence. Raw values remain necessary because normalization itself
answers a different question.

Depth is not a scalar attack amplitude. Additional layers can reinforce or cancel
existing harmonics, so locality need not vary monotonically with $L$. The finite set
$L\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ supports controlled comparisons only; it does not define a continuum
or asymptotic-depth limit.

\subsection{Transport and topological scope}

The common closed-loop transport tests numerical continuity, endpoint sewing, and branch
conventions. In the implemented control the exact reference projector is constant and topologically trivial.
Closure phases are therefore transport diagnostics, not polarization, Wannier centers,
or a topological invariant. The distributed closure is a smooth-gauge construction, not
a localization optimum.

A moving-projector or topologically nontrivial study would require patching, sewing, and
obstruction contracts not present here. Such a study cannot be inferred by replacing the
constant frame with backend eigenvectors while retaining the present evidence semantics.

\subsection{Higher dimensions and nonorthonormal bases}

Fiberwise equations such as $F'U$ and $U^\dagger H'U$ do not intrinsically select a
spatial dimension. Meshes, Fourier indices, inversion closure, and support geometry do.
The present Fourier and locality actions are therefore deliberately one-dimensional. A
2-D or 3-D study must define reviewed tensor or nonorthogonal reciprocal meshes,
multi-index representatives, complete point-group or inversion orbits, and a physical
support metric. No placeholder implementation is supplied.

Likewise, the current frames are orthonormal and their gauge group is $U(r)$. A
nonorthonormal basis requires a positive-definite fiber metric, metric adjoint,
generalized isometry group, conditioning evidence, and a metric-aware Procrustes
objective. Treating that change as an optional parameter of the present action would
hide a new mathematical contract.

\subsection{Right-action alignment hierarchy}

definiteness makes equality rigid. Summing the local inequalities proves the finite-mesh
statement. A known inverse makes every squared norm vanish; nonnegativity and uniqueness
