# Agentic-Development Case Study: Manuscript Record

## Manuscript state

`Evidence collection`

## Working title

**Context Preservation at Coordination Cost: A Longitudinal Case Study of
Human-Governed Agentic Scientific Software Development**

## Candidate central claim

In long-horizon agentic scientific software development, bounded model context
creates a tradeoff between local execution efficiency and global state
preservation. Skills, role-separated subagents, checkpoints, and durable
repository artifacts make task state inspectable and recoverable, but introduce
measurable coordination overhead.

This is a proposition under examination until the required episode evidence and
cross-episode analysis are complete.

## Required evidence

- schema-valid episode records through the completed PI harness incubation;
- reproducible context-management observations under the accepted instrument;
- versioned central table generated from episode records;
- documented defect interception and corrective cycles;
- at least one fresh-session reconstruction or recovery probe;
- explicit unavailable-field reporting;
- independent review of evidence interpretation;
- downstream harness use before claiming amortization or reduced total cost.

## Principal result

The principal result is a cross-episode comparison of coordination load,
context reconstruction, defect interception, corrective work, recovery, and
human disposition.

## Falsification and null result

The stronger effectiveness claim is not supported if explicit context
management adds coordination artifacts and intervention without observable
recovery, defect interception, reduced reconstruction, or successful reuse.
That negative result remains publishable because it identifies conditions under
which a multi-agent harness is excessively granular or unjustified.
