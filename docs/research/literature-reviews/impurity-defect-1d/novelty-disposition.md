# Bounded contribution and novelty disposition

## Disposition

The checked literature contains established precedents for each major component
of the one-dimensional impurity program:

- host-resolvent and finite-rank impurity reasoning;
- weak-defect localization and band-edge effective-mass asymptotics;
- explicit discrete/continuum state-space maps and norm-resolvent limits;
- finite-periodic-volume bound-state shifts;
- multivalley donor envelopes and central-cell corrections;
- Wannier-basis defect contributions and electron--defect matrix elements;
- potential alignment and finite-size analysis in defect supercells; and
- permutation, spin, phase, unitary, and metric-aware alignment of independently
  generated localized Hamiltonians.

Accordingly, no individual component is claimed as new.

## Defensible contribution statement

Within the bounded search, the project contribution is best described as an
**integrated, reproducible controlled diagnostic exercise**. It combines, in one
retained synthetic chain:

1. explicit represented-space compatibility and fail-closed subtraction;
2. known-map and withheld-map recovery with undercomplete-sector
   non-identifiability;
3. separate energy-reference, operator, spectral, wavefunction, and
   model-class errors;
4. independent real-space and folded-fiber extraction routes;
5. an independently implemented finite-rank spectral oracle;
6. separate finite-volume, numerical-support, represented lattice-scale, and
   profile-family studies; and
7. negative controls that remain evidence rather than being replaced by
   reconciled cases.

This statement concerns methodological integration and retained reproducibility,
not priority. The search did not identify one checked source that combines all
seven elements, but absence from a bounded search cannot establish that no such
work exists.

## Materially close sources

Three neighboring strands require especially explicit differentiation:

- **Lihm--Park (2019)** already treats matching of independently generated MLWF
  models through pairing, spin rotation, phase correction, potential alignment,
  and iterative Hamiltonian correction. The project's blind-alignment exercise
  is therefore not positioned as the invention of Wannier gauge matching; its
  distinct purpose is controlled identifiability, undercomplete completion
  freedom, conditioning diagnostics, and structured stopping.
- **Shi et al. (2026 preprint)** uses Hungarian assignment, a dense unitary
  Procrustes rotation, a nontrivial clean/defect overlap metric, and residual
  boundary checks in a first-principles defect-patch workflow. This is close
  contemporary evidence for explicit clean/defect alignment. It has a different
  superconducting BdG objective and does not supply the benchmark's hidden-map,
  route-commutativity, or continuum tests.
- **Kang--Muechler (2026 preprint)** introduces an onsite-only Wannier
  substitutional-defect screening model. It is a useful neighboring model class,
  but it deliberately does not perform full aligned operator extraction and
  reports limitations where hopping, charge redistribution, or spin effects
  matter.

## Claims explicitly declined

The review does not support statements that the project:

- is the first to subtract pristine and defect Hamiltonians;
- invented a Wannier impurity operator, gauge alignment, Procrustes matching, or
  potential alignment;
- introduced finite-rank impurity resolvents, effective-mass theory,
  central-cell corrections, or finite-volume analysis;
- proved an alignment, localization, discrete-to-continuum, homogenization, or
  continuum-crossover theorem;
- established silicon, phosphorus, or boron validity;
- established scientific validation, transferability, or uncertainty
  quantification; or
- completed a comprehensive or systematic literature review.

## Manuscript wording

Recommended wording:

> Established literature supplies the individual ingredients—finite-rank
> impurity resolvents, weak-binding and band-edge reductions, explicit
> discrete/continuum identifications, finite-volume effects, multivalley and
> central-cell semiconductor models, localized-basis defect operators, and
> alignment of independently generated Wannier models. The contribution of the
> controlled one-dimensional program is therefore not a new component theorem
> or material model, but a reproducible integration of these concerns into one
> fail-closed diagnostic chain with separately retained operator, alignment,
> route, finite-size, spectral, and continuum-refinement errors.

Mandatory qualifier:

> This contribution statement is conditional on the bounded search documented
> for 2026-09-17--18 and is not a claim of priority or comprehensive coverage.
