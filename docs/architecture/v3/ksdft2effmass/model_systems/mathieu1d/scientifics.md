# Scientific interpretation

Mathieu1D is a controlled finite periodic model used to expose parent-representation,
band-selection, Fourier-representation, truncation, and independent-verification
behavior. It is not evidence for a semiconductor material and is not a substitute for
the distinct rank-one, rank-two, admissible-set, or higher-rank Periodic1D studies.

The calculation keeps the following questions separate:

1. how two finite parent representations compare with a larger finite plane-wave
   reference at fixed sampled momenta;
2. what selected and adjacent upper-band energies are obtained on the declared training
   and evaluation inventories;
3. whether the complete finite Fourier transform reconstructs its construction mesh;
4. what is discarded by each explicit symmetric hopping range;
5. whether mediated truncation and direct weighted fitting agree on the same finite
   model class; and
6. whether an independent implementation reconstructs every typed result value.

A positive sampled gap is not a proof of continuum band isolation. Decreasing cutoff or
grid discrepancies do not establish an asymptotic convergence rate. The disjoint
evaluation inventory measures withheld finite-sample behavior but is not statistical
validation or an uncertainty estimate. Binary64 route differences are numerical
comparison values, not physical uncertainty.

Connected plot markers denote authored ordered inventories only. Lines do not imply
interpolation, a continuum envelope, or values between samples. Rendering introduces no
new scientific value or decision.

Passing verification establishes agreement between declared production and independent
finite constructions under explicit tolerances. It does not establish continuum
convergence, universal range adequacy, material applicability, publication acceptance,
evidence sealing, release, or human scientific approval.
