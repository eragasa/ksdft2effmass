# Scientific interpretation

A visualization is a presentation of authenticated finite data. It is not an
independent scientific calculation, verifier, proof, uncertainty assessment, retained
execution, or publication decision.

The SHA-256 correlation establishes that the renderer consumed the exact canonical
projection bytes authenticated by the concrete verification. It does not establish that
the projection is physically adequate, continuum-converged, material-valid, complete,
or suitable for publication.

Family owners must state the interpretation and exclusions of every figure. In
particular:

- sampled gaps are not automatically continuum isolation gaps;
- connected finite points are not asymptotic laws or interpolated continua;
- alignment diagnostics are not dimensional reduction;
- a pointwise gauge control is not a general localization algorithm;
- finite-range error curves do not establish material transferability;
- interval whiskers are not statistical error bars unless a statistical owner says so;
  and
- a rendered certificate obligation is not itself a checked theorem.

The root package must remain ignorant of these distinctions. It supplies mechanics that
allow the family verifier and family visualizer to preserve them without duplicating
encoding or authentication behavior.
