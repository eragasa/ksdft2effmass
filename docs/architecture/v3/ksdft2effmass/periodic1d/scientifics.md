# Scientific interpretation

## Bounded purpose

Periodic1D supplies controlled finite model systems for studying how band or subspace
selection, gauge and frame choices, Fourier representation, fitting, and finite hopping
support interact. These calculations isolate methodological behavior before any
material-specific parent is introduced.

The rewrite improves typed composition, independent checking, and evidence correlation.
It does not strengthen the scientific claims of historical retained calculations.

## Family-specific claims

A scalar Fourier-locality result may establish, for its declared finite meshes and
parent representations, reconstruction agreement, hopping coefficients, truncation
behavior, fitting-route differences, and withheld-coordinate discrepancies. It does not
establish that a band is physically isolated merely because the retired historical route
was named `isolated_band`.

A composite alignment result may establish pointwise or sampled spectral equivalence,
frame-transport behavior, block-hopping equalities, and finite-range consequences under
one declared alignment policy. It does not establish a unique gauge, a unique reduced
Hamiltonian, or general locality optimality.

A constrained-admissible-set result may establish that sampled parameter values satisfy
its declared finite spectral and operator thresholds and may retain a bounded separation
certificate. A threshold-sensitivity calculation is post-hoc analysis and remains
separate from a confirmatory admissible-set result.

A higher-rank result may establish behavior only at its declared ranks, meshes,
alignment choices, and model family. It is not inferred from rank two and is not merged
with rank two merely because both use matrix-valued hoppings.

## Nonclaims

No controlled Periodic1D result alone establishes:

- a converged continuum limit;
- uncertainty quantification;
- a material Hamiltonian or material validity;
- successful Quantum ESPRESSO or Wannier90 execution;
- unique Wannier localization or a globally optimal gauge;
- a universal relation between spectral equivalence and finite-range adequacy;
- publication acceptance, evidence sealing, release, or human approval.

A digest establishes only that the checked bytes match. A passing verifier establishes
the declared software and bounded numerical comparisons only. A checked formal
proposition establishes only its stated exact or interval-bounded conclusion under its
recorded hypotheses. Formal proof, numerical verification, integration, retained
evidence, scientific assessment, and publication status remain separate axes.

## Historical evidence

Retained historical M1--M3 bytes, schema identifiers, paths, and publication aliases
remain provenance facts. The successor may cite or compare against them through exact
Git objects and digests, but it must not decode binary64 JSON as exact mathematics,
copy old success labels as current verification, or silently rewrite historical records
into the new layout.
