# Periodic 2-D scientific method

## Two-dimensional representation

The package studies scalar finite-periodic parents indexed by two reduced reciprocal
coordinates and two integer lattice coordinates.

- Direct and reciprocal bases are recorded explicitly.
- A finite represented hopping family is distinguished from an infinite-lattice model.
- Hermitian closure pairs each integer representative with its negative.
- Point-group equivalence is declared by the child rather than inferred from dimension.

## Isotropy and anisotropy

The two children isolate axis-permutation symmetry as a scientific control.

- The isotropic child uses a square-symmetric parent and complete declared symmetry orbits.
- The anisotropic child removes axis-permutation equivalence while retaining declared sign symmetry.
- Equal Euclidean length does not establish equal hopping coefficients in the anisotropic child.
- The children have distinct family identities and interpretation domains.

## Compared evidence

Both children use spectral and represented-operator evidence, while exact definitions
remain child-owned.

- Spectral loss evaluates a frozen two-dimensional reciprocal sample.
- Operator loss evaluates a frozen complete represented block domain.
- A common witness uses one coefficient vector in one maximal parameter space.
- Evaluation and locality observations remain downstream diagnostics.

## Sources and references

This package-level account summarizes child protocols rather than introducing an
external scientific method.

- The isotropic child traces to the retained periodic-2D parent and historical HSH protocol.
- The anisotropic child must identify its exact synthetic parent before scientific freeze.
- Reusable lattice conventions are delegated to reviewed PhysKit contracts.
- Primary references are added by the narrowest child module or class that implements a literature-owned method.
