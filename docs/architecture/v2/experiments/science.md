# Experiment scientific documentation

## Scientific ownership

A concrete experiment owns every scientific choice that changes the question or its
interpretation.

- Parent model and provenance.
- State space, basis, gauge, and energy reference.
- Geometry, units, and boundary conditions.
- Candidate family and parameter embedding.
- Compared channels and sample roles.
- Thresholds, metric, resolution, and evidence premises.
- Bounded conclusions and explicit nonclaims.

## Object distinctions

An experiment must identify objects and maps before fitting, subtraction, or comparison.

- A physical model is not a mathematical operator.
- A mathematical operator is not its finite matrix representation.
- A retained subspace is not a retained operator.
- A represented parent operator is not a reduced candidate model.
- Projection, disentanglement, gauge change, alignment, truncation, and fitting remain distinct operations.

## Evidence roles

Data roles are frozen before decisive evaluation so diagnostics cannot redefine the
question after inspection.

- Source or training data define objectives and admissible sets.
- Evaluation-only data test frozen candidates.
- Withheld data do not tune channels or thresholds.
- Locality and robustness diagnostics remain nondecisive unless a protocol names them as channels.
- Exploratory threshold design remains separate from a sealed run.

## Interpretation boundary

Results retain the validity domain of one frozen experiment version.

- Synthetic controls are not material evidence.
- Material-reference labels do not establish experimental truth.
- Software tests do not establish physical validation.
- Finite calculations do not establish continuum convergence.
- Deterministic thresholds do not establish probabilistic confidence.
- Quantum ESPRESSO and Wannier90 retain ownership of electronic-structure calculation and localization.
