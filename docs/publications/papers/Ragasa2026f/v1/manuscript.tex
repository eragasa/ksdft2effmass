\begin{abstract}
Equal rank of two periodic retained spaces guarantees pointwise unitary maps but does not
guarantee the global periodic, smooth, or symmetry-compatible identification required to
compare their operators. This paper develops a prospective obstruction protocol for
multiband alignment. Periodic projector fields define vector bundles over the Brillouin
torus; local frames are related by transition functions, and an alignment is a relative
bundle isomorphism subject to any additional time-reversal, crystalline, real, or
orientation-preserving structure required by the scientific question. The protocol
distinguishes rank or gap failure, numerical nonresolution, complex-bundle obstruction,
symmetry-equivariant obstruction, and obstruction created only by a restricted real or
$SO(r)$ frame class. It specifies qualified uses of projector Chern formulas,
gauge-invariant lattice curvature, Wilson-loop flow, time-reversal sewing, symmetry
intertwining, and real-bundle diagnostics. A nonidentity Wilson loop, a finite-grid
winding, or a failed gauge optimizer is not by itself classified as topology. When two
nontrivial bundles are nevertheless isomorphic, their operators may be compared through
compatible patchwise representatives even though neither admits the requested global
frame. When the relative bundles are nonisomorphic, no global operator difference is
defined. A flat-band projector grammar constructs globally framed, relatively
isomorphic, complex-obstructed, and category-restricted synthetic controls with exact
rank and gap. A staged controlled-model suite, independent verification, convergence
gates, and fail-closed dispositions are proposed. No model suite or invariant protocol
has yet
been frozen, and no maintained experiment or material classification is reported.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Bloch bundle; topological obstruction; symmetry obstruction;
multiband alignment; Wilson loop; Chern number; time reversal; patchwise frame

\section{Introduction}
A periodic rank-$r$ projector field $P(\mathbf k)$ defines more than a collection of
$r$-dimensional vector spaces. As $\mathbf k$ moves over the Brillouin torus, the fibers
form a vector bundle whose global structure controls whether one can choose a smooth
periodic Bloch frame and, under suitable analytic hypotheses, exponentially localized
Wannier functions~\cite{brouder2007,panati2007}. Local smooth eigenvectors exist across a
sufficiently small gapped neighborhood; the obstruction concerns gluing them into the
global frame required by a chosen representation.

This distinction matters for operator alignment. Paper 5 asks for a periodic unitary map
between pristine and defect retained spaces before subtraction~\cite{Ragasa2026e}. Equal
fiber rank establishes an abstract unitary at each $\mathbf k$, but those unitaries may
fail to glue globally or may fail only after a time-reversal, crystalline, real, or
orientation-preserving constraint is imposed. A numerical seam defect can also arise
from a poor gauge on a trivial bundle. These outcomes require different scientific
dispositions.

Wilson loops, plaquette phases, and sewing matrices are powerful tools, but their
interpretation depends on symmetry class, base space, rank, gap, discretization, and
required frame category~\cite{fukui2005,fukane2006,soluyanov2011,yu2011}. A nonidentity
Wilson loop can occur in a trivial bundle. A zero total Chern number does not establish a
time-reversal-compatible frame. A complex bundle can be trivial while a required real
or crystalline-equivariant framing remains obstructed. Conversely, a failed localization
or phase-smoothing algorithm does not prove any of these obstructions.

This paper defines a hierarchy and fail-closed protocol for those cases. It owns the
escalation path from the topologically trivial controls in Paper 3~\cite{Ragasa2026c} and
the minimal compatibility screen in Paper 5. It does not classify silicon or its dopants
and does not replace the material-specific contract of Paper 7~\cite{Ragasa2026g}. All
numerical model choices and tolerances remain prospective.

\begin{prospective}
\section{Bundle and alignment problem}

\subsection{Projector bundles and local frames}
Let $\mathbb T^D$ be an oriented Brillouin torus and let
\begin{equation}
 P_s(\mathbf k)^2=P_s(\mathbf k)=P_s(\mathbf k)^\dagger,
 \qquad s\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
be periodic rank-$r_s$ projector fields separated from excluded states under a declared
gap or disentanglement contract. Their ranges form complex vector bundles
$\mathcal E_s\rightarrow\mathbb T^D$.

Choose an open cover $\{\Omega_\alpha\}$ and local orthonormal frames
\begin{equation}
 F_{s,\alpha}(\mathbf k):\mathbb C^{r_s}\rightarrow
 \operatorname{Range}P_s(\mathbf k).
\end{equation}
On overlaps,
\begin{equation}
 F_{s,\beta}=F_{s,\alpha}g^s_{\alpha\beta},
 \qquad g^s_{\alpha\beta}:\Omega_\alpha\cap\Omega_\beta\rightarrow U(r_s),
\end{equation}
with cocycle consistency on triple overlaps. A global complex periodic frame exists
precisely when the bundle is trivial in the declared category. Additional regularity is
needed before topological triviality becomes a localization statement; a numerically
smooth finite mesh is not an analytic frame certificate.

\subsection{Relative alignment}
For equal rank $r_0=r_1=r$, an unconstrained global alignment is a bundle isomorphism
\begin{equation}
 U(\mathbf k):\mathcal E_{0,\mathbf k}\rightarrow\mathcal E_{1,\mathbf k}.
\end{equation}
In local frames write
\begin{equation}
 UF_{0,\alpha}=F_{1,\alpha}W_\alpha,
 \qquad W_\alpha:\Omega_\alpha\rightarrow U(r).
\end{equation}
The local matrices glue exactly when
\begin{equation}
 W_\beta=(g^1_{\alpha\beta})^\dagger W_\alpha g^0_{\alpha\beta}
 \label{eq:glue}
\end{equation}
on every overlap. The question is relative: two individually nontrivial bundles may
admit a global isomorphism. Requiring a separate global frame for each would be
unnecessarily strong. Conversely, different bundle classes cannot be made globally
comparable by pointwise polar alignment.

Characteristic data supply necessary compatibility tests and, in carefully stated low-
dimensional categories, may be complete. This manuscript does not assume completeness
outside a selected theorem domain. Matching sampled Chern or Wilson diagnostics alone is
not a general proof of bundle isomorphism.

\subsection{Patchwise operator comparison}
Let $H_{s,\alpha}=F_{s,\alpha}^\dagger H_sF_{s,\alpha}$. When Eq.~\eqref{eq:glue} holds,
define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta H_\alpha
 =H_{1,\alpha}-W_\alpha H_{0,\alpha}W_\alpha^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The matrices $\Delta H_\alpha$ transform on overlaps by conjugation with
$g^1_{\alpha\beta}$ and represent one global endomorphism of $\mathcal E_1$. Its
spectrum, traces, and unitarily invariant fiber norms are global even when no single
frame is admitted. A conventional global Wannier matrix and one globally labeled hopping
table are not available in that obstructed frame category.

If the bundles are not isomorphic, arbitrary local differences do not glue into one
operator. They may diagnose where compatibility fails, but they are not pieces of a
global impurity operator. This prevents ``patchwise comparison'' from concealing a
relative obstruction.

\subsection{Required category}
An obstruction is meaningful only relative to a requested structure:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{complex periodic}: frames and maps take values in $U(r)$;
 \item \textbf{time-reversal compatible}: an antiunitary $\Theta$ and its square are
 fixed, with a declared Kramers sewing rule;
 \item \textbf{crystalline equivariant}: maps intertwine selected unitary or antiunitary
 space-group representations;
 \item \textbf{real}: a real structure is fixed and transitions lie in $O(r)$; or
 \item \textbf{oriented real}: transitions and alignments lie in $SO(r)$.
\end{enumerate}
Failure in a narrower category does not imply failure in a broader one. If the comparison
only requires a complex unitary, a real-frame obstruction is inapplicable. If time
reversal or a crystalline representation is mandatory, discarding it changes the
problem and requires an explicit human decision.

\subsection{Controlled constructions with guaranteed properties}
Obstruction tests require positive and negative controls whose bundle properties do not
depend on the tested numerical classifier. A common parent grammar starts from any
periodic rank-$r$ projector $P(\mathbf k)$ with $Q=I-P$ and defines the flat-band
Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
 H_P(\mathbf k)=E_-P(\mathbf k)+E_+Q(\mathbf k),
 \qquad E_+-E_-=\Delta>0.
 \label{eq:flat-projector-parent}
\end{equation}
Hermiticity, rank, periodicity, the retained projector, and the isolation gap $\Delta$
are then exact construction guarantees. The topology is carried by $P$, not created by
diagonalizing sampled values of $H_P$.

Four related routes construct the required comparison classes.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Globally framed control.} Choose a periodic orthonormal frame $F$ and set
 $P=FF^\dagger$. This guarantees a complex-trivial bundle and a supplied global frame.
 Paper 3's periodic-unitary grammar is a higher-rank source of such controls.

 \item \textbf{Relatively isomorphic control.} Start from any $P_0$, including an
 analytically certified nontrivial one, choose a periodic ambient unitary $V$, and set
 \begin{equation}
  P_1=VP_0V^\dagger,
  \qquad U=VP_0.
  \label{eq:constructed-relative-isomorphism}
 \end{equation}
 Exactly $U^\dagger U=P_0$, $UU^\dagger=P_1$, and $P_1U=UP_0=U$. Hence $U$ is a
 supplied relative isomorphism even when neither bundle has a global frame. If local
 frames are used, their matrices $W_\alpha$ obey Eq.~\eqref{eq:glue} by construction,
 and patchwise operator differences form one global endomorphism.

 \item \textbf{Complex-obstructed control.} Choose $P_0$ and $P_1$ with analytically
 certified unequal characteristic data in a domain where that mismatch is a valid no-go
 result. Over a two-dimensional Brillouin torus, $C[P_0]\ne C[P_1]$ is sufficient to
 forbid a global complex relative isomorphism. For rank-one two-level controls, a smooth
 periodic nonvanishing field $\mathbf d_s$ supplies
 \begin{equation}
  P_s=\frac{I-\widehat{\mathbf d}_s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbf d}_s=\frac{\mathbf d_s}{|\mathbf d_s|},
  \label{eq:constructed-two-level-projector}
 \end{equation}
 and its Chern class is fixed by the degree of $\widehat{\mathbf d}_s$ under the frozen
 orientation and sign convention. Equal certified degree can seed a relative control;
 unequal degree seeds an obstruction control.

 \item \textbf{Category-restricted control.} Supply a complex isomorphism as in route 2,
 but choose exact symmetry sewing or real transition data with a separately proved
 incompatibility in the narrower category. Inequivalent representation content at a
 symmetry-invariant momentum, a certified time-reversal invariant mismatch, or a
 qualified real characteristic-class mismatch can then forbid the corresponding
 equivariant, real, or $SO(r)$ map without forbidding the supplied complex map. The
 applicable theorem and its completeness domain are part of the construction, not
 inferred from optimizer failure.
\end{enumerate}

These routes separate what is guaranteed from what remains measured. Analytic formulas,
transition-function cocycles, or theorem-qualified invariants certify the intended rank,
gap, bundle class, and relative disposition. Numerical execution verifies the serialized
implementation and measures discretization, link conditioning, branch stability,
constructive residuals, and convergence under mesh refinement and gauge attacks. A
sampled integer or a failed frame search cannot replace the independent certificate.

The grammar is not an assertion that arbitrary topology, symmetry representations,
spectra, and locality can be combined independently. They must satisfy projector
regularity, cocycle, symmetry, and representation-compatibility conditions in one finite
state space. Global periodic frames realize only trivial bundles, while nontrivial cases
require projectors or patchwise frames with certified transition data. Nor does an exact
synthetic class imply material realism. Paper 5 uses the trivial or relatively admitted
side of this grammar to authorize subtraction; Paper 6 owns the controlled obstruction
and patchwise cases. No Paper 3 or Paper 5 mesh, threshold, result, or evidence status is
transferred by this lineage, and no particular C0--S1 model is selected here.

\section{Obstruction hierarchy and diagnostics}

\subsection{Rank and isolation precede topology}
Topology is assigned only after constant rank and the defining isolation or
entanglement-stability condition pass on the full parameter domain. A rank change or gap
closing is reported directly. In a sweep it may locate a candidate phase boundary, but
no invariant is assigned at the singular point unless nodal classification is separately
in scope.

Numerical inability to resolve a small gap, a rank-deficient neighbor overlap, or a
mesh-dependent projector is \textbf{NUMERICALLY\_UNRESOLVED}, not topological evidence.

\subsection{Complex Chern obstruction}
For a sufficiently regular two-dimensional projector and frozen orientation, define
\begin{equation}
 C[P]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb T^2}
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(P[\partial_{k_x}P,\partial_{k_y}P]\right)
 \,d^2\mathbf k.
\end{equation}
A nonzero first Chern number obstructs a global smooth periodic complex frame and the
corresponding orthonormal exponentially localized Wannier representation under the
standard hypotheses~\cite{brouder2007,panati2007}. For relative alignment,
$C[P_0]\ne C[P_1]$ proves that the two complex bundles are not isomorphic. Equality is a
compatibility condition, not a universal certificate in arbitrary dimension or symmetry
class.

On a mesh, the proposed primary calculation uses gauge-invariant link determinants or
projector Bargmann loops. For frame overlaps
$M_\mu(\mathbf k)=F^\dagger(\mathbf k)F(\mathbf k+\hat\mu)$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_\mu(\mathbf k)
 =\frac{\det M_\mu(\mathbf k)}{|\det M_\mu(\mathbf k)|}
\end{equation}
only when the magnitude passes a frozen conditioning gate. Oriented plaquette phases
yield a lattice Chern sum~\cite{fukui2005}. An integer-looking finite sum is admitted as
evidence only with a gap record, orientation and branch convention, independent
projector-route reconstruction, gauge attacks, mesh refinement, and agreement with an
analytic or certified control where available.

For three-dimensional families, Chern calculations on two-dimensional slices are
reported slice by slice. A slice invariant may change only where assumptions fail; it is
not automatically a complete three-dimensional classification.

\subsection{Wilson loops}
A non-Abelian Wilson matrix is constructed from unitary polar factors of conditioned
neighbor-overlap matrices along a closed loop. Its eigenphases are invariant as a set
under periodic gauge transformations. The protocol retains path, base point,
orientation, sewing closure, eigenphase matching, degeneracy handling, and refinement.

A nonidentity Wilson matrix or nonzero Berry phase does not establish an obstruction.
Even a trivial bundle can have nontrivial connection holonomy. Topological use requires
a qualified spectral-flow or winding statement tied to the applicable invariant and
symmetry~\cite{alexandradinata2014}. Ordinary closure failures are first tested as
numerical or gauge-sewing defects.

\subsection{Time-reversal-compatible obstruction}
For fermionic time reversal, $\Theta^2=-1$ and the retained rank is even. A local frame
defines the sewing matrix
\begin{equation}
 w_{mn}(\mathbf k)=\langle u_m(-\mathbf k)|\Theta u_n(\mathbf k)\rangle.
\end{equation}
The protocol verifies the antiunitary representation, Kramers degeneracy at time-
reversal-invariant momenta, reciprocal sewing, and required projector covariance. A
vanishing total Chern number does not establish a global Kramers-compatible frame. The
$\mathbb Z_2$ channel must use a reviewed gauge-invariant formulation, such as time-
reversal polarization, Pfaffian/sewing data, or qualified Wilson-loop partner switching,
with cross-method agreement where assumptions overlap
\cite{fukane2006,soluyanov2011,yu2011}.

A time-reversal obstruction can coexist with an unconstrained complex frame. The correct
disposition is \textbf{GLOBAL\_COMPLEX\_BUT\_TR\_OBSTRUCTED}, not ``no global frame''
without qualification.

\subsection{Crystalline and fragile obstruction}
If a space-group or point-group action is required, the bundle and alignment must be
equivariant. At invariant momenta and lines, retained representation content and
compatibility relations provide necessary tests. A proposed relative map must intertwine
the two sewing representations. Topological quantum chemistry makes these distinctions
systematic~\cite{bradlyn2017}.

Stable, fragile, and obstructed-atomic-limit statements remain separate. A fragile
Wannier obstruction can disappear after adding selected trivial bands~\cite{po2018}.
Such an enlargement changes the retained space and cannot be performed silently to pass
an alignment gate. Representation matching at finitely many special momenta is not
assumed sufficient unless a theorem for the declared symmetry and band class is in the
protocol.

\subsection{Real and orientation-preserving restrictions}
An antiunitary symmetry squaring to $+1$, or another reviewed real structure, can reduce
the gauge group from $U(r)$ to $O(r)$. Requiring oriented real frames further restricts
it to $SO(r)$. Orientation and higher real-bundle obstructions may be probed through
Stiefel--Whitney data, transition-function determinants, and qualified Wilson
diagnostics~\cite{ahn2019}.

These channels are evaluated only when the real structure and gauge restriction are
part of the contract. The protocol records whether $w_1$, $w_2$, or another quantity is
complete for the declared dimension and rank. A complex-trivial bundle must not be
called topologically obstructed without naming the narrower real or symmetry category.

\section{Prospective controlled-model protocol}

\subsection{Model roles}
The initial study should select the smallest exact models needed to distinguish:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{C0}: a gapped complex-trivial family with a supplied global frame;
 \item \textbf{C1}: a rank-one Chern family with topological and trivial controls;
 \item \textbf{C2}: same-nonzero-Chern copies or deformations, testing a relative map
 despite absent individual global frames;
 \item \textbf{C3}: equal-rank families with different Chern class;
 \item \textbf{T0/T1}: time-reversal-trivial and nontrivial even-rank zero-total-Chern controls;
 \item \textbf{R0/R1}: complex-trivial controls trivial or obstructed under a declared real restriction; and
 \item \textbf{S0/S1}: crystalline-equivariant compatible and incompatible controls.
\end{itemize}
No model family is selected here. Standard two-dimensional Chern models, including
Qi--Wu--Zhang and Haldane families~\cite{qi2006,haldane1988}, are candidates only after
their state spaces, parameters, energy units, analytic boundaries, and publication roles
are reviewed. A first immutable version may cover only C0--C3; later symmetry classes
would receive separate versioned protocols.

\subsection{Required analytic contracts}
Each case must state:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item base torus, orientation, reciprocal coordinates, and boundary identifications;
 \item finite Bloch state space, matrix ordering, units, and exact Hamiltonian;
 \item target rank and analytic or independently certified gap domain;
 \item required frame category and symmetry representations;
 \item known invariant or transition functions and sign convention;
 \item expected relative-alignment disposition;
 \item singular parameter values excluded from invariant claims; and
 \item the distinction between theorem, analytic certificate, and sampled diagnostic.
\end{enumerate}
Models with different state spaces or energy scales are not treated as approximations of
one another and their errors are not pooled.

\subsection{Numerical and verification design}
A frozen mesh ladder must refine independently in each reciprocal direction and avoid
unqualified conclusions at analytic singularities. The primary route computes spectra,
projectors, conditioned links, plaquette invariants, Wilson data, transition functions,
and constructive alignment residuals. A deterministic periodic $U(r)$ frame attack is
applied to every case.

An independent verifier reconstructs each Bloch operator from the serialized contract
and uses projector traces or Bargmann invariants rather than importing primary
eigenvectors or link routines. It checks Hermiticity, ranks, gaps, periodicity, seam
projectors, integer and branch stability, gauge-attack invariance, analytic expectations,
transition cocycles, relative gluing, and constructive frames where expected.

A failed optimizer is never the obstruction certificate. Positive evidence is the
qualified invariant or incompatibility of transition data; constructive failure is a
secondary consistency check.

\section{Discussion}
The relative formulation changes the practical question. Operator comparison does not
always require trivializing each bundle independently. If two nontrivial bundles are
isomorphic, an alignment can exist and a global abstract operator difference can be
represented consistently in patches. What fails is the requested single global frame or
its Wannier representation, not necessarily the comparison itself.

Symmetry adds a second distinction. A complex alignment may exist while a
Kramers-compatible, crystalline-equivariant, real, or orientation-preserving alignment
does not. Reporting only ``topological'' would hide which scientific requirement caused
the failure. The ordered obstruction ladder keeps that requirement visible.

Finite-mesh calculations require caution. Lattice Chern formulas can be exactly integer
valued on a fixed mesh, and Wilson phases can show convincing flow, while the target gap,
link conditioning, branch convention, or continuum refinement remains unresolved.
Integer output is one channel in a certification protocol, not an automatic theorem.

The proposed scope also prevents topology from becoming an explanation of last resort.
Rank mismatch, state misassignment, optimizer-basin variation, discontinuous input data,
and incorrect reciprocal sewing all have prior diagnostics. Only after those alternatives
are excluded should the workflow escalate to bundle classification.

\section{Conclusions}
This paper defines a prospective hierarchy for global multiband alignment. It
distinguishes complex bundle isomorphism from individual frame triviality, then separates
symmetry-equivariant and restricted real-frame requirements. Projector invariants,
transition functions, Wilson flows, and constructive maps have qualified roles; no
single diagnostic is allowed to prove every obstruction.

The next step is a reviewed C0--C3 synthetic protocol with exact model contracts, a
frozen refinement ladder, independent projector verification, transition-function
checks, and immutable evidence identities. Time-reversal, crystalline, and real classes
should enter only through separately frozen successors. No material or dopant calculation
is required to establish these controlled mathematical boundaries.
