\section{Continuum effective-mass reduction}
\index{continuum embedding}
\index{crossover radius}

The continuum stage introduces host band-edge tensors and channel data, a
multivalley donor solver for phosphorus, and a multiband acceptor solver for
boron. Screened-Coulomb and central-cell model families supply candidate
continuum impurity terms. These are mathematical models with their own state
spaces; their matrices cannot be subtracted directly from lattice operators.

A continuum-to-Wannier embedding is therefore required. Let
\begin{equation}
  J:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{cont}}\longrightarrow\mathcal S_{\mathrm W}
\end{equation}
be the declared embedding over its validation domain. A continuum operator
$H_{\mathrm{cont}}$ is represented in the atomistic comparison space through
an explicitly defined construction such as
\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{cont}\rightarrow W}=JH_{\mathrm{cont}}J^{\dagger},
\end{equation}
with normalization, channel ordering, spatial discretization, boundary
conditions, and any non-isometric behavior of $J$ stated. Synthetic-envelope
tests verify the implementation before first-principles claims are attempted.

The proposed crossover radius $r_{c,d}$ is the smallest tested radius beyond
which both exterior and cross-coupling criteria satisfy their declared
tolerances. If no tested radius passes, the result is recorded as
$r_{c,d}=+\infty$ over the tested domain. A finite crossover is not assumed in
advance.
